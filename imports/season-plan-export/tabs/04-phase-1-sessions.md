# Phase 1 session plans

Session-by-session plans for the eleven Foundations weeks (4 October to 17 December 2027): the joint session, plus the 1s split and a suggested 2s split for the 2s captains. Every drill names its source so coaches can look up the full version.

## How to use these plans

Each week lists three learning points, a timed joint session and both split sessions. Times are guides: stop a drill just before players have had enough (Hildo), and move a group on as soon as it has the point.

**Every session**

- Announce the theme and three learning points at the start; ask players to say them back at the end.
- Warm-up is the Herd standard warm-up: 60% runs with backward, skip and low-shuffle returns; then mobility (lunge and reach, knee hugs, figure-4, drinking bird, quad stretch, side lunges, T-spine rotations, penguin walk); then nervous-system work over 15 m (cycle kicks, lateral high-knee skips, lateral skips, falling leaf, toe-touch skips) (HN Practice Sessions, Standardised Warm Up). It has the same loosen, dynamic, fast shape as Hildo's (HB App. A.3), so wherever a table says "warm-up", use this. Rotate who leads it.
- Focused throwing is in mixed groups of 4-5, with a 1s player in each group and one technique point of the week (DK 5.2.1). Pick three or four steps from the 15-step Focused Throwing menu (two discs flat, vary pivots, target and catch, slight IO/OI, half-pivot backhands, scan and throw, blades, off-hand, push passes, scoobers, hammers, quick releases) (HN 10 Big Drills).
- Explain the drill and its rotation in under two minutes; experienced players demo it, and a coach adds the finer points while it runs (DK 5.2.3, HB App. A.4).
- Water breaks every two to three activities (HB App. A.5).
- Coach by questions in games: "What did you notice? Where was the space?" (IF).

* **Warm-up finisher: 4v3 mini.** Four on offence, three on defence, on a small pitch (about 30 x 20 m with endzones), with groups of 10-11 per pitch. Each four gets one possession: they score, or turn it over, then jog off while the next four start from their back line. Swap the defenders every few minutes. It gives quick reps and lots of touches, and the offence always has a free player to find; it fits the IF asymmetric-offence idea. Use it at the end of the warm-up whenever the session allows, and before every tournament game (Kieran).

**Numbers:** plans assume around 40 players at a joint session. Most drills run as four parallel groups of about 10; small-sided games use 4-5 pitches of about 30 x 20 m.

**Kit:** 40+ discs, 60+ cones, bibs in three colours, 4 pool noodles (week 10), tennis balls (week 9), a phone for filming the 5-10-5 test, and a printed test sheet.

## Week 1 · 4 Oct · Welcome, baseline tests, grip and pivot

**Learning points:** (1) Balance: weight on the non-pivot foot, no extra step after the throw. (2) Your pivot gives you 360 degrees of threat; use it. (3) Two hands on the disc whenever you can.

**What to tell players**

- **Why fundamentals first:** HB recommends fundamentals at the start of every season, experienced players included, and repeating the key subjects regularly because one-time teaching doesn't stick (HB App. A.1).
- **Stability:** step into the throw with most of your weight on the non-pivot foot, stay upright, and don't need an extra step afterwards. Being off balance costs accuracy, and a lifted pivot foot is a travel. If someone leans forward, have them pivot at 90 degrees to the target and hold the non-throwing arm out for balance (DK 4.4, 4.8.4).
- **Say:** "Step, flick, point." The arm traces a straight line to the target and finishes pointing at it, not above the shoulder (DK 4.3, 4.8).
- **The pivot gives you 360 degrees of freedom:** step closer to the marker for quick flat throws, further away for wider arcs (BFD ch. 1).
- **1s vocabulary:** dump = any pass behind the disc line; mac = upline cut from level with or above the disc; zip = vertical cut for a short throw on the move; dynamic dump = a dump caught on the closed side that flows quickly to the break side. Call them out loud. "The time spent training these fundamentals will never be enough" (BFD 1.2).
- **2s: the Five Frisbee Habits:** sprint your cuts; cut up and down, not across; check behind you when you go deep; look for the dump by stall 5; only throw to people who are free. "It is never a bad idea to use the dump" (DK 6.11).
- **Rules for experienced players with newcomers:** no Ds on newcomers, throw to them, don't poach, no stall (DK welcome session).
- **Ask:** "What challenges does a thrower face getting space to throw? Can the defender stop everything? Which pivot foot gets more range?" (Flik)

**Coaching notes**

- "It is never a bad idea to use the dump": at worst it is a neutral reset (DK 6.11).
- Set the reset handler negative (behind, about 45 degrees) on the open side and positive (ahead, about 30 degrees) on the break side (BFD).
- Keep the baseline tests quick and positive: the point is the March comparison, not today's number.

**From the Herd notebook**

- **Where am I now?** Show the rookie → substitute → starter → baller ladder and ask each player to place themselves skill by skill (throwing, cutting, defence); you can be a starter thrower and a rookie cutter. Revisit at the February benchmark (HN Amsterdam Conference, From Rookie to Baller).
- **Process goals, not just outcome goals:** goals that can be hit several times a session, such as "check your shoulder 5-6 times a point" or "give sideline talk three times a point" (HN Amsterdam Conference, Process over Outcome).
- **Home position:** both hands on the disc, lined up with your centre. Every pivot, fake and half-pivot throw starts from here (HN Amsterdam Conference, Breaking a Defensive Heart).
- **For coaches:** aim for the zone of proximal development, where mistakes happen but not on every rep. If players are bored or everything goes perfectly, add challenge; if every rep is a turnover, regress (HN Coach Corner, Zone of Proximal Development).

## Week 2 · 11 Oct · Pivots and fakes

**Learning points:** (1) A fake has to look like a real throw: full arm, eyes and shoulders. (2) One decisive pivot beats three half-pivots. (3) Catch in threat position: square to the field, ready to throw upfield.

**What to tell players**

- **Fakes must look like real options.** One decisive early move beats lots of small, non-threatening fakes. Decide based on how the marker is set up (DK, Breaking the mark).
- **The break-side space is only behind the marker:** either an early IO break, or fake and go into a late around (DK).
- **Threat position:** as soon as you catch, set yourself as if about to make a specific throw, with the right grip and pivot. It has to be credible, so the marker must choose. Or stay neutral with two hands on the disc to give the marker no reference (BFD ch. 1).
- **Keep the marker busy while you read the field.** Pivot without losing sight of the field, and don't fake when you can just throw (HB App. B).
- **On the sideline:** if you're at risk of getting stuck, all focus goes on getting the disc off it (HB Tr 13). A 270-degree trap pivot finds the dump (UAP Forehand).
- **Ask:** "What makes a fake believable? How does a thrower's balance affect their range and control?" (Flik L2). "How does faking one type of break make the other more available?" (Flik L3 Lesson 3)

**Coaching notes**

- Fakes are there to unbalance the marker and create space for a simpler throw (BFD ch. 1).
- Release point is the same for the fake and the throw; if the fake is lazy, the marker learns to ignore it (Plan).
- Coach the 2s to accept a neutral dump from the sideline rather than force a line throw.

**From the Herd notebook**

- **The shimmy:** fake the first six inches of a pivot, rock your shoulders and keep a throwable grip. If the mark doesn't bite, finish the pivot and throw. Pivoting costs you sight of the field; the shimmy doesn't (HN Point of Attack, The Shimmy).
- **Pivot directly left or right,** never straight forward or back; that's where the channel behind the force opens (HN Amsterdam Conference).
- **One great move plus a counter:** "it only takes one great move to break a mark", but once you have it, build the counter so the mark has to respect both (HN Amsterdam Conference).
- **Show then throw:** sometimes just showing the disc moves the mark. Throw from grounded legs, with two hands on the disc for as long as you can (HN Flik, Breaking the Mark Quick Tips).
- **Sideline traps:** the goal isn't only to escape but to punish the defence for committing to the sideline. Square up to the middle; cutters attack the break side and middle (HN Handler Situations).

## Week 3 · 18 Oct · Catching

**Learning points:** (1) Feet first: get your body behind the disc before your hands. (2) Two hands whenever you can (pancake or crab). (3) Catch the edge that is spinning towards you, and watch it all the way in.

**What to tell players**

- **The catching sequence:** move your feet first, splay your fingers, clap the centre or leading edge, bring the disc in (UAP Catching, Shardlow).
- **Three catches:** clap (pancake), crab and one-handed. Always two hands where possible. Aim for ideal technique 9 times out of 10 and use the other 1 in 10 for variety (UAP Catching).
- **Catch the edge spinning towards you** (UAP Catching).
- **For throwers:** aim at the top of the chest, "at the basket" (UAP Catching).
- **Say:** "Never say sorry before the disc hits the ground" (HB App. B).
- **Handler rule 1, for the look-forward drill:** on the catch, throw forward or make a convincing forward fake, then dump straight away. Step away from your marker for the dump, not towards the dump (HB Tr 13).
- **Ask:** "What was different about catching standing still and catching on the move?" (Flik L1). After boxing out: "Is this harder on defence or offence? Why?" (Flik L4)

**From the Herd notebook**

- **Attack the disc with a hard step as you catch.** The notebook's line: "If you are never dropping these discs then you are not trying hard enough" (HN Handler Offense, Moment of Catching).
- **Run through the disc:** in go-to drills, don't slow down when you catch; drops are fine as long as you sprint through (HN 10 Big Drills, Go-To Attack).

- **Winning in the air:** read the throw, gain and hold body position, go up with your outside hand (HN Next Gen 10, Winning Discs in the Air).

## Week 4 · 25 Oct · Acceleration and change of direction

**Learning points:** (1) Small steps and a chop stop: stay low to change direction. (2) Five or six hard steps sell the fake to the defender. (3) Commit: a half-cut is worse than no cut.

**What to tell players**

- **Accelerate with many small steps.** In the chop stop, stay low for a second, then commit to one side (HB Tr 14).
- **Say:** "A cut of 2-3 steps fakes the thrower, 5-6 steps fakes the defender", so never make short cuts (HB App. B).
- **The goal of cutting is separation.** There are three variables: speed, direction and position. Three cut families: *explosion* (change speed to press an advantage you already have), *commitment* (drive until the defender commits their hips, then change direction), *shoulder* (attack the defender's shoulder and read it) (UAP Cutting, Raynor).
- **Five ways to get open:** rush, jab, drive, wait, seal (UAP Handler O, Wiggins). Close the buffer before a jab step; the seal is not plan A (Flik L4 Lesson 3).
- **Say:** "It's the cutter's job to get free for the thrower. Few bad throws, many bad options" (DK).
- **For coaches:** agility is nervous-system work, so keep volume low and rest full; don't let it turn into conditioning (UAP Changing direction, Witmer).
- **Ask:** "How did you convince the defender to go the wrong way, rather than just trying to run past them?" (Flik L2). "Which cut was your favourite? Which was most effective? Which one do you want to improve?" (Flik L4 Lesson 3)

## Week 5 · 1 Nov · Throwing to space

**Learning points:** (1) Throw to the space, not the person. (2) Huck flight: IO early, flat through the middle, OI to finish, so the disc floats into space. (3) Time the throw to the cutter's turn, not their arrival.

**What to tell players**

- **Lead the receiver:** throw into the space they are running into, not at where they are now (DK 9.12; Flik L1).
- **Ideal huck flight:** IO, then flat, then OI into the receiver. Practise at 110% of your range, because overthrowing is better than underthrowing (Flik L4 Lesson 4).
- **Huck checklist:** extra-tight grip; weight on the non-pivot foot; arm kept close to the body; disc around waist height with the elbow at 90 degrees and the other arm out for balance; maximum wrist flick and a little IO so it doesn't roll over; finish pointing at the target (DK 4.7).
- **Build range gradually:** start close and move apart. Technique doesn't change just because the distance does (DK 4.4). In wind, release low going upwind, high going downwind, and add spin (DK 4.9).
- **Timing:** start your cut before the thrower catches ("moment -2/-3"), so you're open as they look (BFD).
- **We need deep and under cuts:** only cutting under makes us easy to mark. Deep cuts go straight downfield, unders at an angle, and check behind you when you go deep (DK, session on triangle marking and cuts).
- **Why flow matters:** if the disc keeps moving, the defence is out of position a large amount of the time (DK).
- **Ask:** "How is throwing to space different from throwing at a moving target?" (Flik L4 Lesson 4). "How can a player plan ahead to receive a pass in the future?" (Flik L3 Lesson 6)

- "Throw to space" is the root of retention: a receiver running into space is easier to hit than a static one (DK 9.12).

**From the Herd notebook**

- **Herd's own hucking session** (HN Practice Sessions 9): "Hucks are not about throwing as fast and hard as you can", they're about control. Archery scoring: +2 in stride, +1 overthrown, 0 if the cutter waits. Practise 10% beyond your range.
- **Ideal reception:** the disc fades towards the cutter and slows down, arriving at catchable height; a disc fading away speeds up and is much harder to catch (HN Flik, Ideal Reception).
- **Backhand huck:** step out flat or even backwards, not forwards; rotate fully; release flat or slightly IO (HN Next Gen 10).
- **Accuracy comes from spin:** don't guide the disc; drive it through the target with a full follow-through (HN Excel Premium, Accuracy).
- **Deep cut timing:** wait for power position, start shallow, then be decisive. Practise it with the Dishy Huck drill (HN Next Gen 10; HN 10 Big Drills).

## Week 6 · 8 Nov · Clearing and space

**Learning points:** (1) Your clear is a cut: run it with purpose. (2) Make space before you take it. (3) Know where active, inactive and ready space is on every possession.

**What to tell players**

- **Three kinds of space** (UAP Cutting, Raynor, after Martin Aguilera): *active* space is what our offence intends to use to advance the disc; *inactive* space is what we don't intend to use; *ready* space is where we wait to set up. In a vert stack, the open side is active, the break side inactive and the stack is ready space. These change as the disc moves.
- **Make space:** clearing is always space-making; you vacate active space for a teammate. Think of yourself as part of the cutters, not an individual cutter (UAP Raynor).
- **Take space:** counter what a teammate creates. For example, a deep cut draws a defender and the under cutter waits for the space it opens (UAP Raynor).
- **Make your clear a viable cut,** and never cut along grid lines (UAP Digital mentor, Boyle).
- **Cut even if you won't get open:** it tires your defender and they lose their sense of the field (HB App. B).
- **1s, ho spacing:** "space decides the role, not the role the space" (BFD, field roles).
- **Ask:** "What different spaces can be attacked at the same time? How does this change as a player moves?" (Flik L3 Lesson 4). "How does the movement and positioning of other players affect one person's ability to get open?" (Flik L3 Lesson 5)

## Week 7 · 15 Nov · Marking base

**Learning points:** (1) Marking base: wide, low, weight on the balls of the feet. (2) Shuffle, never cross; load the inside leg. (3) Take away the dangerous throw first, then close the other.

**What to tell players**

- **Marking base:** an athletic stance with weight on the balls of the feet, hips back, chest open and eyes on the thrower (UAP Marking, Wiseman). Feet shoulder width; shuffle, don't lunge (DK).
- **To change direction, load the inside leg** without the knee buckling (UAP Wiseman).
- **Triangle mark:** "shown throw with hand, around with body, inside with shoulder" (UAP Wiseman). Against big pivots, step back to cover the throw, then close in again (DK, triangle marking).
- **Priorities:** take away the dangerous throw first, then close (DK). The first five seconds of pressure are what make throwers give up on the break side (BFD).
- **Hands:** DK says to keep them unpredictable, while UAP Wiseman argues for intentional hands that fit the team's strategy. For our triangle mark, hands are intentional: the hand shows the throw we allow.
- **The mark's job continues after the release:** stop the give and go. The sideline calls the marker's hands (DK).
- **Ask:** "Were defenders able to resist the temptation to move around too much when forcing? How does it feel as a thrower?" (Flik L2 Lesson 7). "What effect did better footwork have on the strength of the force?" (Flik L4 Lesson 7)

## Week 8 · 22 Nov · Defensive footwork

**Learning points:** (1) Low and balanced, on the balls of your feet. (2) Never turn your back on the disc: drop step and open the hips. (3) Choose the space you're protecting and buffer to it.

**What to tell players**

- **Say:** "Low, fast feet, head up, pick your spot" (UAP Defensive footwork, Matthews).
- **Don't turn your hips or back:** the attacker will attack your shoulder to turn your hips. Run progressively: sidestep, then crossover, then sprint (DK).
- **Defence is about space:** compress receivers into the area you want, disrupt their easiest sprint path, and close the passing lane rather than chasing (BFD).
- **Defensive priorities:** the team agrees an order of spaces to protect. Flik suggests newer teams protect the open under first, then deep, then break (Flik L4 Lesson 6). Our back-shoulder match D protects deep first and concedes the under, so tell the 2s which order they're using.
- **Buffering:** keep the "string from belly button to belly button" (Flik L4 Lesson 6).
- **Keeping margins:** "do this every training at high intensity and players will be in great shape for D" (HB Tr 22).
- **Ask:** "Why is it important to use footwork to maintain your defensive priorities?" (Flik L4 Lesson 8). "What happens when people get away from the team objective? How does a team build trust in its system?" (Flik L2 Lesson 8)

## Week 10 · 6 Dec · Wide and low releases

**Learning points:** (1) Width and a low release beat the mark. (2) Hold the finish for 2 s to prove your balance. (3) IO early or around late: choose and commit.

**What to tell players**

- **Extended mark:** the goal is to get point-blocked sometimes so you find your limits; never throw the same throw twice in a row (UAP Breaking the mark, Saul). Look to the break side early in the stall, and make the break lead to yardage-gaining continuation (UAP Saul, film study).
- **1s stretch:** pick one or two unorthodox throws to master (UAP Saul, "cheeky stuff").
- **Every touch should advance the disc, change the point of attack, or break the mark** (BFD, performance model).
- **Ask:** "When is it too early or too late to throw? Is it easier to throw short or long on around throws?" (Flik L3 Lesson 1). "Did you notice each other pivoting wider or throwing lower during Get 10? Were you aware of your own?" (Flik L4 Lesson 1)

**New vocabulary**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Dump | Any pass behind the disc line (BFD) |
| Mac | An upline cut from level with or above the disc (BFD) |

| Dynamic dump | A dump caught on the closed side that flows quickly to the break side (BFD) |
| Positive / negative reset | A reset set up ahead of the disc (break side) or behind it (open side) (BFD) |

| Threat position | Catching in a credible throwing stance so the marker has to choose (BFD) |
| Shimmy | A fake of the first few inches of a pivot (HN) |
| Half pivot | Releasing before the non-pivot foot lands (HB) |
| Extended mark | Breaking drill: two discs, stall 4, two pivots, no repeated throw (UAP) |
| Cutting through | A break at about 75 degrees that lets the receiver keep running on the break side (BFD) |
| Active / inactive / ready space | Space we're attacking / not using / waiting in to set up (UAP) |

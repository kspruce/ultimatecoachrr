# Classroom sessions

A series of short off-pitch sessions, online or in person, that bring the ideas from *The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee*, *The Culture Code* and *The Captain Class* (plus the Rise Up talks, Flik and BFD) to the whole club. Each one is timed so the idea is used on the pitch the following week.

## How they run

- **Length:** 45-60 minutes. Online sessions run on a non-training evening; in-person sessions run straight after training or at a social.
- **Who:** open to both squads unless marked otherwise. Optional but encouraged; record online sessions for anyone who misses them.
- **Who leads:** a captain or player leader, rotating, with a guest coach where useful. Running a session is a good first leadership job (Flik, Shared Leadership).
- **Pre-work:** never the whole book. Send a one-page summary of the idea in the leader's own words, one short clip or question, and an invitation to read the chapter if they want to.
- **Shape:**

| Time | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Hook | A story, a clip or a quick challenge |
| 0:05 | The idea | One idea, no more than three points, with an ultimate example |
| 0:15 | Small groups | Groups of 3-5 (breakout rooms online) discuss two or three questions |
| 0:35 | Share back | Each group shares one thing; the leader writes them up |
| 0:45 | Commitment | Everyone names one thing they'll try at training this week |

- **Rules that make it work** (The Culture Code):
  - Everyone speaks at least once.
  - The leader admits a mistake or doubt early on.
  - No idea is shot down in the room.
  - The write-up goes to the whole club within two days.
- **Link to the pitch:** the following session's coach mentions the idea and the commitments, and the session's catchphrase or cue word gets used in drills.

## Phase 1: Foundations

### C1 · Week 3 (w/c 18 Oct) · Online · How we learn: the Heckler and the Hero

**Goal:** everyone understands why Herd trains through games and questions, and why mistakes are welcome.

**Hook:** Gallwey noticed his tennis students improved more when he said less. Ask: "When did you last play your best, and what were you thinking?"

**The idea:**

- **The Heckler** judges, doubts and overthinks.
- **The Hero** is your trained body and instincts.
- We train so the Hero can take over in games. That's why sessions are mostly games, coaches ask more than they tell, and mistakes are part of learning (*The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee*; Ian French).

**Small-group questions:**

- What does your Heckler say most often on the pitch?
- When have you been "in the zone"? What was different?
- What helps you learn: being told, being shown, or working it out?

**Activity:** *Heckler translation.* Each player writes three things their Heckler says and rewrites each one as something the Hero would say.

**Commitment:** keep a short inner game journal after each session this week.

**On the pitch:** week 3 is catching. Coaches ask "what did you notice?" rather than correcting, and players use "soft hands" as a cue word.

### C2 · Week 7 (w/c 15 Nov) · In person after training · Belonging: what makes groups click

**Goal:** build the welcome and belonging habits we want for the whole season, while new players are still joining.

**Hook:** Coyle's spaghetti-and-marshmallow challenge. Teams of four get about 15 minutes, 20 sticks of spaghetti, tape, string and a marshmallow to build the tallest tower with the marshmallow on top. Coyle reports that kindergartners beat business students because they try things together instead of jockeying for status.

**The idea:**

- **Belonging cues** show energy, treat the person as an individual, and signal a future together. Together they say "you're safe here".
- **One "bad apple"** can drag a group down; good apples lift it (*The Culture Code*).

**Small-group questions:**

- What made you feel welcome, or not, when you joined Herd?
- What small things could we do at every session to make new players feel part of it?
- What does a "good apple" do at training?

**Activity:** agree three welcome habits for the rest of the season, such as introducing yourself to anyone new, throwing with someone you don't know, and saying thank you after a drill.

**Commitment:** say two specific thank-yous to teammates this week.

**On the pitch:** week 7 is marking. Use the new welcome habits, and pair a newer player with an experienced one in the triangle-mark drills.

### C3 · Week 10 (w/c 6 Dec) · Online · Captains, player leaders and anyone interested · Leaders, not stars

**Goal:** agree what leadership looks like at Herd before the 2028 season, and which player-leader roles we need.

**Hook:** ask "Who's the best captain you've played with, and what did they actually do?"

**The idea:**

- Walker's seven traits of the greatest captains (*The Captain Class*).
- The four myths from the Rise Up talk *Leadership without an Ego*.

**Small-group questions:**

- Which of the seven traits do you see in our club already? (Behaviours, not names.)
- Which jobs at Herd are thankless, and who does them?
- What leadership roles are we missing? Think about warm-ups, the sideline, stats, socials and welcoming new players.

**Activity:** draft a list of player-leader roles with one-line descriptions, and agree how people will be nominated (Flik, Shared Leadership).

**Commitment:** each person takes on one thankless job at the next session.

**On the pitch:** roles start after the winter break. Captains for 2028 are confirmed with these traits in mind (Season plan, Leadership).

## Phase 2: Pressure and trials

### C4 · Week 14 (w/c 3 Jan) · Online · Process over outcome: our priorities for 2028

**Goal:** share the season's goals openly and turn them into process goals players can hit every session. This doubles as BFD's pre-season meeting.

**Hook:** "Winning Nationals" isn't something you can do on a Wednesday night. What can you do on a Wednesday night?

**The idea:**

- **Outcome goals** set the direction; **process goals** give feedback every session (Rise Up, *Process over Outcome*).
- **Name and rank your priorities,** and be ten times clearer about them than feels necessary (*The Culture Code*).
- **One strategic anchor per phase** (Rise Up, *Army of Dump Defenders*).

**Small-group questions:**

- What would make this season a success for you, and for your squad?
- Which three things matter most this season, in order?
- What's one process goal you could hit at every training session?

**Activity:** each group ranks the draft team priorities. Captains merge them and publish the final ranked list.

**Commitment:** one personal process goal for the next month, shared with a buddy.

**On the pitch:** the Phase 2 anchor (breaking the mark) is announced at the restart session.

### C5 · Week 17 (w/c 24 Jan) · Online · Performing under pressure, before trials

**Goal:** give everyone tools to perform at trials and in big games.

**Hook:** the Ring of Fire reframe, "I'm not nervous, I'm excited". The energy is the same; the direction changes (Flik Case Studies, Ring 2021).

**The idea:**

- **Some arousal helps:** aim for relaxed concentration.
- **Treat pressure as a challenge,** not a threat.
- **Use breathing, visualisation and a pre-point routine** (*The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee*; Flik, Getting out of your head).

**Small-group questions:**

- What happens to your body and thoughts when you're nervous?
- What do you do now before a big point? Does it help?

**Activity:**

- Each player builds a 10-second pre-point routine: one breath, one cue word, one picture of a good rep.
- Finish with a 5-minute guided visualisation of a trial game going well.

**Commitment:** use the routine at every trial.

**On the pitch:** at trials, assessors note good decisions after mistakes, not just the mistakes.

## Phase 3: Early season

### C6 · Week 25 (w/c 20 Mar) · In person, by squad · How we review: candour and after-action reviews

**Goal:** set up how each squad reviews events before Tour 1, so reviews are honest without being harsh.

**Hook:** Coyle says "I screwed that up" are the most important words a leader can say. The captain goes first with a real mistake from Solent.

**The idea:**

- **The after-action review:** what did we intend, what happened, why, what will we keep, what will we change?
- **Candour, not brutal honesty;** bad news is delivered in person.
- **Judge decisions against the realistic alternative,** not the outcome (*The Culture Code*; Rise Up, *Developing Great Defenders*).

**Activity:** run a practice review on two Solent clips or the Solent stats. Then agree the squad's review routine for events: five minutes at the end of each day, and the full review at the following training.

**Commitment:** at Tour 1, everyone brings one "keep" and one "change" to the debrief.

**On the pitch:** this sets up the Phase 3 event loop and the fix menu.

### C7 · Week 29 (w/c 17 Apr) · Herdlodge · Purpose: values, a catchphrase, one bar-setting behaviour

This is the values workshop already planned for the Saturday afternoon at Herdlodge (Phase 3, week 29), framed as a classroom session.

**The idea:**

- **Purpose is built from a few clear priorities,** simple catchphrases and one behaviour that sets the bar, like Quinnipiac's "Forty for Forty" (*The Culture Code*).
- **Identity is something you live,** not just a list of words (Rise Up, *Everybody In*).

**Activity:**

- Players bring their own values; groups merge them and the club votes down to 3-7.
- For each value, agree what it looks, sounds and feels like.
- Finish with one catchphrase and one bar-setting behaviour.

**On the pitch:** the catchphrase and behaviour are used in every session and briefing from week 30.

### C8 · Week 34 (w/c 22 May) · Online, by squad · Watching the best: film session

**Goal:** see the concepts we've trained done at the highest level, and practise watching with awareness.

**Clips:**

- **1s:** Molly Brown's defence comeback (poaching lanes, switching on deep cuts) and Brute Squad's resets with momentum.
- **2s:** Fury's sideline resets and Japan U24's zone.

All are from Flik's Deep Dives and Team Analysis.

**How to watch:**

1. Each group watches one player through a clip (*The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee*, awareness).
2. Then they answer: what did they notice before the play happened?

**Small-group questions:**

- What's one thing we already do like this team?
- What's one thing we could steal for Tour 3?

**Commitment:** each player picks one habit from the clips to try at the next session.

**On the pitch:** week 34 is tempo and zone offence over the top; the switching and reset clips back up weeks 32-33.

## Phase 4: Regionals

### C9 · Week 38 (w/c 19 Jun) · Online, by squad · Scouting and game plans

**Goal:** everyone understands Plans A, B and C and how scouting works, before the scouting cards are due in week 40.

**Hook:** CUSB put their 2019 EUCF result down to scouting. They had a team of scouts, two watching each game (Flik Case Studies, CUSB 2019).

**The idea:**

- BFD's game-plan checklist (our team, their team, context, conditions).
- Plans A, B and C, and the triggers for switching.
- The one-word calls.

**Activity:**

- Scout one opponent clip together using the card template.
- Hand out opponents to pairs of scouts.

**Commitment:** scouting cards are due in week 40.

**On the pitch:** week 38's split session rehearses Plan A and Plan B; week 40 presents the cards.

### C10 · Week 41 (w/c 10 Jul) · In person, 30 minutes before training · Competing with heart: flow, Spirit and big games

**Goal:** go into Regionals calm, together and spirited.

**The idea:**

- **Flow** comes when challenge matches skill, goals are clear and attention is in the present.
- **Compete with heart:** top-level competitiveness and Spirit together (*The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee*).
- **Great captains switch off negative emotions fast** and lift teams with wordless displays (*The Captain Class*).

**Activity:**

- Agree the squad's Spirit intention for Regionals (Flik, Practicing Spirit).
- Practise the reset routine (one breath, one cue word, next job) in pairs.

**Commitment:** one wordless way each player will lift a teammate at Regionals, such as a high five, a point or eye contact.

**On the pitch:** the "what if" scrimmage straight after uses the reset routine.

## Phase 5: Nationals

### C11 · Week 46 (w/c 14 Aug) · Online · 1s · Nationals mindset

**Goal:** a short, calm, shared picture of how the 1s want to play at Nationals.

**The idea:**

- **Identity:** PoNY rallied around being the best defensive team (Flik Case Studies, PoNY 2018).
- **The squad's catchphrase and bar-setting behaviour** from Herdlodge.
- **Doggedness and emotional control** (*The Captain Class*).

**Activity:**

- A guided visualisation of the Nationals game-day routine.
- Each player names their role in one sentence ("play your role").

**Commitment:** the process goals for Nationals.

**On the pitch:** the week 47 rehearsal uses the same routine.

### C12 · Week 51 (w/c 18 Sep) · In person at the season social · Season review: what we learned

**Goal:** celebrate, learn, and keep players for next season.

**The idea:** an after-action review of the whole season, and overdoing the thank-yous (*The Culture Code*).

**Activity:**

1. Groups answer the five review questions about the season.
2. Everyone answers Abrashoff's three questions anonymously: what do you like most, what do you like least, and what would you change if you were captain?
3. Finish with thank-yous and awards.

**On the pitch:** the answers feed the captains' review and next season's plan.

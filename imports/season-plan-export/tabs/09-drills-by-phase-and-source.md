# Drills by phase and source

A one-page summary of each phase and every drill and game the sessions use, grouped by source, so coaches can find a drill quickly or swap one for another.

## Coaching preference: Ian French games first, then Flik drills

When planning or adapting a session, look in this order:

1. **Ian French (IF) games** for the main block of every session (game, short drill, game). They follow the constraints-led approach: change the rules, numbers, space or scoring so players find the solution themselves, and coach by asking questions rather than giving instructions. IF still uses drills for 20-30% of the time.
2. **Flik drills** for that short drill block. Flik has a lesson series, a theory library and a drill library of 218 short drills in the Herd notebook (Flik Drills section), most with video.
3. **Other sources** for technique detail and fitness: BFD (shared vocabulary, S&C, game plans), DK's Bible (throwing technique), Hildo Bijl (drill catalogue), the Ultimate Skills Project, Kieran's Session Plans and the rest of the Herd notebook.

**How the lists work:** each drill is listed under the source the session table cites; where a row cites several sources, it's listed under its main one. Week numbers are in brackets. Warm-ups, water breaks, free games and recaps are left out. The Herd standard warm-up and the 4v3 mini (Kieran) run every week.

**Source key:** IF = Ian French, *Games for Coaching Ultimate Frisbee* · Flik = Flik lessons, theory and drill library ("HN Flik" = Flik pages in the Herd notebook) · BFD = Bologna Flying Disc coaching programme · DK = DK's Bible · HB = Hildo Bijl · UAP = Ultimate Skills Project · SP = Kieran's Session Plans · HN = Herd OneNote notebook.

## Phase 1: Foundations (weeks 1-11, Oct-Dec)

Open, skills-only sessions that welcome new players. The phase builds throwing, catching and resets, then getting open, then marking and footwork, then breaking the mark, and ends with a festive mixed tournament. Split sessions build the shared vocabulary and the basics of each squad's offence.

## Phase 2: Pressure and trials (weeks 14-24, Jan-mid Mar)

Skills now face a real defence. Breaking the mark under pressure, the dominator, reading the pitch and team defence come in before the three trials, which are built from games the club already knows. Then escaping the sideline, quick processing, the Solent warm-up, and the Bull zone as squads are announced.

## Phase 3: Early season (weeks 25-37, mid Mar-mid Jun)

The playbook is built through test events, with an event loop: light event weeks, then fixing the top problems the week after. Aerial skills, zone offence (around, through and over), the endzone, hucking, Herdlodge values, wind, pulling, the dominator against poaches, switching, the Bull zone and tempo.

## Phase 4: Regionals (weeks 38-43, mid Jun-Jul)

No new structures: scouting, Plans A, B and C, O- and D-lines and mental skills, with one fundamental refresher a week. The 2s learn the arrowhead in full. Regionals, then EUCS Summer (1s only).

## Phase 5: Nationals (weeks 44-51, Aug-Sep)

Recovery, three weeks to rebuild and peak, a taper, then Nationals (and Euros if the 1s qualify). No new material. The 2s finish with end-of-season games and awards, then act as the 1s' rehearsal opponents. The season ends with benchmarks, reviews and awards.

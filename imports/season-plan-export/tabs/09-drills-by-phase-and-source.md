# Drills by phase and source

A one-page summary of each phase and every drill and game the sessions use, grouped by source, so coaches can find a drill quickly or swap one for another.

## Coaching preference: Ian French games first, then Flik drills

When planning or adapting a session, look in this order:

1. **Ian French (IF) games** for the main block of every session (game, short drill, game). They follow the constraints-led approach: change the rules, numbers, space or scoring so players find the solution themselves, and coach by asking questions rather than giving instructions. IF still uses drills for 20-30% of the time.
2. **Flik and GRUT drills** for that short drill block. The 9 GRUT drills are filmed with Flik and listed in their own section below. Flik has a lesson series, a theory library and a drill library of 218 short drills in the Herd notebook (Flik Drills section), most with video.
3. **Other sources** for technique detail and fitness: BFD (shared vocabulary, S&C, game plans), DK's Bible (throwing technique), Hildo Bijl (drill catalogue), the Ultimate Skills Project, Kieran's Session Plans and the rest of the Herd notebook.

**How the lists work:** each drill is listed under the source the session table cites; where a row cites several sources, it's listed under its main one. Week numbers are in brackets. Warm-ups, water breaks, free games and recaps are left out. The Herd standard warm-up and the 4v3 mini (Kieran) run every week.

**Source key:** IF = Ian French, *Games for Coaching Ultimate Frisbee* · Flik = Flik lessons, theory and drill library ("HN Flik" = Flik pages in the Herd notebook) · BFD = Bologna Flying Disc coaching programme · DK = DK's Bible · HB = Hildo Bijl · UAP = Ultimate Skills Project · SP = Kieran's Session Plans · HN = Herd OneNote notebook · GRUT = GRUT (Amsterdam) drill videos, filmed with Flik Ultimate.

## New games written for this plan

These games are Herd's own, written where the libraries had nothing that fitted. Each is in the drill library under the same name, so a session activity of that name links to it.

### Jedi lanes game

Jedi is a dump and swing that gains yards: every throw that loses yards is followed by a change of the point of attack. This game makes that worth points.

- **Weeks:** 36.
- **Set-up:** A full-width pitch about 40 m long with endzones. Cone lines split it lengthways into three lanes: left, middle and right. 7v7, or 5v5 on a narrower pitch.
- **Rules:** Normal ultimate with a one-way force. A pass that loses yards (a dump) is free. The pass after a dump must be caught in a different lane from the one the dump was thrown from. If it isn't, play goes on but the Jedi point can't be scored on that possession.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 2. A "Jedi" scores 1 straight away: a dump, then a pass into another lane that is caught upfield of where the dump was thrown from. The defence scores 1 for a turnover in the lane the disc was dumped from. Play to 9.
- **What the rules push players to do:** Going backwards only pays if the disc then goes somewhere new. Handlers look for the swing as the dump is in the air, and cutters learn to attack the lane the swing is heading for.
- **1s version:** Stall 6, even numbers, and the Jedi only scores if the swing is caught upfield of the original thrower.
- **2s version:** Stall 8, 7v6 or 5v4. Any dump followed by a pass into another lane scores the point, so the dump and swing itself is rewarded.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: Stall 5; two Jedis needed before a goal counts. Easier: A spare attacker; two lanes instead of three.
- **Ask:** "After the dump, where was the space?" "Who moved first to make the swing possible?" "When did a reset gain yards, and what made that happen?"
- **Source:** Plan; Bologna Flying Disc, Jedi

### Double-team trap game

A sideline trap made worse than any real one, so that squaring up and resetting early become the obvious answer.

- **Weeks:** 21.
- **Set-up:** 5v5 on a 30 x 20 m pitch with endzones. A cone line 3 m inside each sideline marks the trap zones.
- **Rules:** Normal ultimate at stall 8. When the disc is in a trap zone, a second defender may join the mark until stall 6, then must leave. Walk the square-up and the two-player handler reset through once before playing.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 1. A goal from a possession that got out of a double team within two passes scores 2. A stall or a turnover in a trap zone scores 1 for the defence.
- **What the rules push players to do:** With two markers on the disc, someone is free. Throwers learn to square up to the middle and find them; the reset and cutters learn to attack the break side and the middle rather than the line.
- **1s version:** Joint session game. With stronger groups the double team may stay until stall 8.
- **2s version:** With newer groups the second defender leaves at stall 4, or the offence plays 5v4.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: The double team stays until stall 8. Easier: The second defender leaves at stall 4.
- **Ask:** "With two markers on you, where was the space?" "Who was left free, and how did they show you?" "What did the team gain by getting off the line in two passes?"
- **Source:** Plan; HN Handler Situations; HN Excel Premium

### Deep-shot game

A long, narrow pitch that gives deep cutters room and makes the huck to an isolated cutter the best play.

- **Weeks:** 28.
- **Set-up:** A pitch the full length but half the width, with endzones. 5v5 (1s) or 5v4 (2s).
- **Rules:** Normal ultimate with a one-way force. Any throw may score, but the pitch is too narrow for lazy swings.
- **Scoring:** A goal from a huck over 30 m caught in stride scores 3. Any other goal scores 1.
- **What the rules push players to do:** Throwers look long when a cutter is isolated and hold the disc when they aren't. Deep cutters wait for power position, start shallow and commit.
- **1s version:** 5v5, and from week 45 the huck must go to the break side to score 3.
- **2s version:** 5v4, and a huck over 25 m scores 3.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: The huck must be thrown to the break side. Easier: A spare attacker; a shorter huck counts.
- **Ask:** "When was the deep shot on? What told you?" "What set the huck up: a swing, a dish, or power position?" "Deep cutters: when did you start, and what were you watching?"
- **Source:** Plan; after Ian French, long- and short-throw bonus points

### Mark-call game

Switching between the triangle mark and the flat mark on a sideline call, under real throws.

- **Weeks:** 9.
- **Set-up:** 4v4 (then 5v5) on a 30 x 20 m pitch. One caller per team on the sideline.
- **Rules:** Normal ultimate, starting in the triangle mark. Once in each possession the defending team's caller shouts "triangle" or "flat". The mark must change at once and downfield defenders adjust to the new open side. In the harder round the call can come twice.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 1. The defence scores 1 if the mark has changed within a second of the call, and 1 for a stall or a block.
- **What the rules push players to do:** The mark practises the switch while a thrower is trying to break it, and the sideline practises calling early enough to be useful.
- **1s version:** The 1s' game in week 9.
- **2s version:** The 2s don't need the flat mark yet. Their version is the tram-line game with sideline calls.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: The call comes at stall 4, or twice in a possession. Easier: The call comes before the first throw.
- **Ask:** "What did the thrower get in the second before you changed?" "Downfield: how did you know the open side had moved?"
- **Source:** Plan; Bologna Flying Disc (flat mark)

### Back-shoulder game

The 2s' version of Space Invaders: one priority only, deep first.

- **Weeks:** 8.
- **Set-up:** 4v4 on a 35 x 20 m pitch with endzones. Person defence, one-way force.
- **Rules:** Normal ultimate at stall 8. Defenders start every cut on the cutter's deep, open-side shoulder.
- **Scoring:** An under caught costs the defence nothing. A deep catch (past the defender who started behind) scores 2 for the offence. A block or a stall scores 2 for the defence. A goal scores 1.
- **What the rules push players to do:** Defenders are paid for giving up the under and stopping the deep, which is the 2s' chosen order of priorities. Cutters learn that the under is what this defence gives.
- **1s version:** The 1s play Space Invaders, with all four priorities scored.
- **2s version:** The 2s' game in week 8.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: Stall 7, even numbers. Easier: A spare defender playing deep.
- **Ask:** "Which shoulder were you on when you were beaten?" "What did you give up on purpose?"
- **Source:** Plan; after Flik, Space Invaders

### Cut-and-clear game

Clearing is part of cutting. In this game the next cut can't start until the last cutter has cleared.

- **Weeks:** 6.
- **Set-up:** 5v5 on a 35 x 25 m pitch in a vertical stack.
- **Rules:** Normal ultimate at stall 8. A cutter who isn't thrown to must clear to the break side before anyone else cuts. A throw to a cutter who went before the lane was clear is a turnover.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 1. A goal caught by a player who cleared earlier in the same possession scores 2.
- **What the rules push players to do:** Players run their clear with purpose because the team is waiting for it, and they learn that a clear becomes the next cut.
- **1s version:** The 1s play vert from the front with the same idea: a goal only counts if the scorer cleared at least once.
- **2s version:** The 2s' game in week 6.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: Stall 6; clears must go through the break side and back to the stack. Easier: 5v4.
- **Ask:** "Where do you go when you aren't thrown to?" "How did you know the lane was yours?"
- **Source:** Plan; UAP Cutting (Raynor)

### Sideline trap game

Every possession starts trapped, and the bonus is for punishing the trap rather than surviving it.

- **Weeks:** 21.
- **Set-up:** 5v5 on a 30 x 25 m pitch. The disc is checked in on a sideline with a trap mark set and three handlers.
- **Rules:** Every possession starts on the trap sideline. The defence may call "shift" at stall 5 to shut the dump.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 1. An escape to the break side or the middle within two passes scores a bonus point straight away. A stall on the line scores 1 for the defence.
- **What the rules push players to do:** Handlers get the disc off the line before the defence is balanced, and cutters attack the break side and the middle.
- **1s version:** The 1s' game in week 21.
- **2s version:** The 2s play the sideline-start game: a goal scored without going back to the starting sideline scores 2.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: The dump defender may poach the first look. Easier: No shift call.
- **Ask:** "At what stall did you turn to the dump?" "Where did the cutters go to help?"
- **Source:** Plan; SP Vertical Stack; HN Handler Situations

### Sideline-start game

The 2s' sideline game: the hardest place to start, over and over, usually with a spare attacker.

- **Weeks:** 2, 21 and 40.
- **Set-up:** 5v4 or 5v5 on a 30 x 20 m pitch; later 7v7.
- **Rules:** Every possession starts on a sideline, and the first pass must leave the line. Stall 7 or 8.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 1. A goal scored without the disc going back to the starting sideline scores 2. In week 40 a give-and-go off the line scores a bonus.
- **What the rules push players to do:** The dump or the power-position pass is always on, so players learn to take it early instead of forcing a throw up the line.
- **1s version:** The 1s play the 3-handler game and the sideline trap game.
- **2s version:** Weeks 2, 21 and 40.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: 5v5 at stall 6. Easier: 5v3, or stall 10 for the first pass.
- **Ask:** "What was your first look on the sideline?" "What did a neutral dump give you?"
- **Source:** Flik L3 Lesson 8; Herd's scoring (Plan)

### Forward-backward stall game

The 2s' first reset game: turning to the dump buys time.

- **Weeks:** 1 and 14.
- **Set-up:** 4v4 or 5v4 on a 30 x 20 m pitch with endzones.
- **Rules:** The stall is 4 if you throw forwards and 10 if you throw backwards. The marker counts to 10; a forward throw after "four" is a turnover.
- **Scoring:** A goal scores 1. From week 14 a completed dump and swing scores a bonus point.
- **What the rules push players to do:** Players find out for themselves that the dump is never a bad idea, and start looking for it by stall 4.
- **1s version:** The 1s play positive and negative resets, 2v2.
- **2s version:** Weeks 1 and 14.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: Stall 3 forwards. Easier: 5v4.
- **Ask:** "What changed when you turned round?" "What did the dump give your team?"
- **Source:** Flik L3 Lesson 7; DK's Bible 6.11

### 1v1 from the stack spot

The 1v1 cutting test played as a game, with a live mark on the thrower. Getting open under pressure for the cutter; throwing under pressure for the thrower.

- **Weeks:** 14, 16 and 21.
- **Set-up:** Fours: thrower, marker, cutter, defender. The cutter starts on a stack spot 15 m from the thrower with the defender on their back shoulder.
- **Rules:** The cutter has until stall 6 to get open, under or away. The thrower has a live mark and may break it. Rotate one place after every rep.
- **Scoring:** A clean catch scores 1 for the cutter. A stop (a stall, a block or an incomplete throw) scores 1 for the defender. First to five.
- **What the rules push players to do:** The cutter has to be open while the throw is still possible, and the thrower has to deliver it past a mark. It also means every player has met the trial test's set-up several times before it counts.
- **1s version:** Stall 6; in week 21 the disc starts on the sideline and an under only counts if it is caught off the line.
- **2s version:** Stall 8, and the defender starts a step off.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: Stall 5; the defender is told under or away. Easier: The defender starts two steps off.
- **Ask:** "What did the defender take away, and what did that leave you?" "Thrower: when did you know which way they were going?"
- **Source:** Season plan, Measuring improvement; Flik L4 Lesson 3; HN 10 Big Drills (Win the Box)

### Out-of-timeout game

Every possession starts from a timeout, so the set play and the defence against it get many reps.

- **Weeks:** 40.
- **Set-up:** 7v7 on a full pitch.
- **Rules:** Each possession starts from a timeout in one of two spots: mid-pitch at stall 7, or on the endzone line. The offence runs its out-of-timeout play. The defence may double the reset or poach the nearest lane for three seconds.
- **Scoring:** Five possessions each; the offence aims to score 3 out of 5.
- **What the rules push players to do:** The offence designs its play expecting a poach or a double team, and the defence practises taking the first option away for three seconds.
- **1s version:** The 1s' game in week 40.
- **2s version:** Optional for the 2s, with their endzone call only.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: The defence chooses its look after seeing the set. Easier: The defence plays straight person D.
- **Ask:** "What did the defence do in the first three seconds?" "What was the second option, and who knew it?"
- **Source:** Plan; HN Coach Corner, Time Outs

### Dominator 3v3

The dominator as a small game: yards gained by give-and-go count as a goal.

- **Weeks:** 16 and 32.
- **Set-up:** 3v3 on a 20 x 15 m pitch.
- **Rules:** Stall 5. Play starts at one end; the offence works the disc upfield with short passes and give-and-go.
- **Scoring:** A 15 m gain made with give-and-go passes scores as a goal. In week 32 a fourth defender poaches, and a pass to the player the poacher left scores a bonus.
- **What the rules push players to do:** The thrower has to move the instant the disc leaves their hand, and the three have to keep the throws stable while moving.
- **1s version:** Weeks 16 and 32.
- **2s version:** The 2s play inside break, then continuation (week 16) and 3v3 possession (week 32).
- **Harder / easier:** harder: A fresh defender every point; a poacher. Easier: 3v2.
- **Ask:** "How can two or three players working together help the team move the disc?" "When the poacher came, who was left?"
- **Source:** Plan; Bologna Flying Disc, Dominator; Hildo Bijl 34.2

### Inside break, then continuation

One reliable break has to lead to the next throw.

- **Weeks:** 16.
- **Set-up:** 3v3 on a 25 x 20 m pitch with endzones, one-way force.
- **Rules:** Stall 8. Normal play, but see the scoring.
- **Scoring:** A goal only counts if the possession had an inside break followed by a pass to a different cutter.
- **What the rules push players to do:** The thrower breaks to the receiver's outside shoulder so they can keep running, and the next cutter goes as the first one catches.
- **1s version:** The 1s play Dominator 3v3.
- **2s version:** The 2s' game in week 16.
- **Harder / easier:** harder: Stall 6. Easier: 3v2.
- **Ask:** "Where was the next cutter when the break was caught?" "What made the break easy to continue from?"
- **Source:** Plan; HN 10 Big Drills (Break to a Point); Flik L3 Lesson 2

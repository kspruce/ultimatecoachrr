# Phase 2 session plans

Session-by-session plans for the eleven Pressure and trials weeks (3 January to 19 March 2028): the joint session, plus the 1s split and a suggested 2s split for the 2s captains. Everything from Phase 1 now meets a live defender, the main structures arrive, and the three trials reuse games the club already knows. Every drill names its source.

## How Phase 2 works

The same rules as Phase 1 apply. The source key (IF, BFD, DK, HB, UAP, Flik, SP, HN), the Herd standard warm-up, focused throwing and "three learning points, said back at the end" are all set out at the top of the Phase 1 sessions tab. This tab adds what changes once the pressure goes on.

**What changes in Phase 2**

- **Every drill gets a live defender** once it has been walked through. Stall counts drop: the 1s play stall 5-6 in drills and games, the 2s stall 7 (Weekly plan).
- **The split session gains a team-toolkit block at 2:00**, where the structures are taught: horizontal stack and dominator for the 1s, vertical stack for the 2s, and team defence for both (Season plan, Weekly structure).
- **Split sessions run as two provisional groups** until squads are picked in March, and players can move between them (Season plan).
- **Trials (weeks 18-20)** are built from games the club already knows, so they reward improvement and decisions rather than familiarity with a system. The method (each assessor grades one attribute, scores are ranked and then discussed) is in the Season plan under Recruitment (HN Coach Corner, Tryouts).
- **Fun consequences stay in split sessions only.**

* **The 4v3 mini warm-up finisher continues** (rules in the Phase 1 tab). In Phase 2, add a live mark and drop the stall to 7, so it doubles as resets-under-pressure practice.

**Structure vocabulary for this phase**

- **Horizontal stack (ho), 1s main offence:** three handlers and four cutters spread across the width (about 9 m apart), 8-12 m from the disc, so throwing long is easy. The two cutters in front of the disc are the active pair; the others stay wide. Learn the ho shapes (away and under, peppermills) rather than vert shapes (HN Flik, Horizontal Stack; BFD, "Alternate pistons").
- **Dominator, 1s:** two or three handlers work the disc upfield with short passes and give-and-go while the cutters clear vertically; one player gets every second pass (BFD, Dominator; HB 34.2; HN Team Analysis, PoNY 2021).
- **Vertical stack (vert), 2s main and 1s second offence:** a handler, a dump and a stack starting about 10 m from the disc, with cuts in the lanes either side. Cut from the back (the 2s default), or from the front with a third handler ("3H") as an upfield reset (HN Flik, Vertical Stack and Upfield Dump).
- **Team defence:** person defence with back-shoulder positioning and hard-working forces, plus poaching and clogging near the disc. The 1s also learn which spaces are dangerous and when to poach; the 2s use poach and clog to close gaps on stronger teams (Season plan, Defence toolkit).

**Numbers:** about 40 club players, plus trialists in weeks 18-20. Plans assume four or five 30 x 20 m pitches for small-sided work, and two full pitches for 7v7.

## Week 14 · 3 Jan · Restart: resets under a live mark

**Learning points:** (1) Look forward once, then engage the dump by stall 4-5. (2) Every reset needs two threats. (3) A reset should create a new angle, not just lower the stall count.

**What to tell players**

- **The new phase:** everything from the autumn now meets a live defender. Trials start in four weeks and use the games we already play.
- **Two threats:** "Whenever you're trying to get rid of your defender in an Ultimate match, you always need to have (at least) two threats." A dump on the closed side threatens across the field and upfield; a dump on the open side threatens a short pass backwards and upfield (HB 25.1, 25.4).
- **The forbidden region:** catching behind the thrower on the trap sideline solves nothing; use it only as a last resort to reset the stall (HB 25.3).
- **Dump and swing:** the thrower squares up to the reset; the reset takes a step upfield to commit the defender, then comes back into the backfield away from the sideline. Swing cutters go late rather than early (HN Handler Offense, Dump Swing).
- **1s rule:** every throw that loses yards must be followed by a change of the point of attack (BFD, Jedi).
- **2s:** refresh the Five Frisbee Habits: sprint your cuts, cut up and down, check behind you, look for the dump by stall 5, only throw to free players (DK 6.11).
- **Ask:** "What does a team gain by turning and passing backwards?" (Flik L3 Lesson 7)

## Week 15 · 10 Jan · Breaking the mark under pressure

**Learning points:** (1) Catch in threat position. (2) Break early, while the mark is still setting. (3) Off-balance and half-pivot throws are skills we train.

**What to tell players**

- **Threat position:** set yourself as soon as you catch, so the marker has to choose; or stay neutral with two hands on the disc (BFD ch. 1).
- **Break early:** "When a mark initially establishes itself, it is particularly vulnerable to being broken" (HN Flik, Scoring via Upfield Dump).
- **Half pivot:** release before the non-pivot foot lands (HB 35.1). Off-balance throws while bumped or mid-step are worth practising on purpose (UAP Breaking the mark, Saul).
- **Breaks for continuation:** throw to the receiver's outside shoulder, wider than expected (HN Flik, Breaking the Mark).
- **1s, ho stack:** three handlers, four cutters spread across the width, 8-12 m from the disc. Only the pair in front of the disc is active; the rest stay wide and out of the lanes (HN Flik, Horizontal Stack).
- **Ask:** "When is it too early or too late to throw?" (Flik L3 Lesson 1). "How does faking one type of break make the other more available?" (Flik L3 Lesson 3)

## Week 16 · 17 Jan · Beating a good mark; the dominator

**Learning points:** (1) Threaten both sides so the marker has to choose. (2) After the throw, move straight away. (3) After one break, look for the next break immediately.

**What to tell players**

- **The one-crossover game:** the thrower may bring the disc across their midline only once. Breaking without a pivot is a win for offence, with a pivot it's a tie, and the defence wins on a turnover or two pivots. If you only have 10 minutes a week for marking, this is the one (UAP Handler O and D, Wiggins).
- **Dominator:** one player gets every second pass. When you catch, immediately fake or throw; after you throw, start running, ideally before the disc has left your hand (HB 34.2).
- **Give-and-go footwork:** immediate, delayed, break-side, and the Sockeye move (after an around break, the receiver charges and dishes back). Be smooth rather than rushed: if you get called for travels, you're rushing (HN Handler Offense, Give-Go Moves).
- **Why give-and-go works:** the mark sits on one side and downfield defenders cover the other, so a thrower who follows their throw across the receiver's face attacks space nobody is guarding (HN Flik, Give and Go).
- **Ask:** "How can two or three players working together help the team move the disc?" (Flik L3 Lesson 10)

## Week 17 · 24 Jan · Reading the pitch: free players and dangerous spaces

**Learning points:** (1) Scan before you catch. (2) Know which spaces are dangerous for the disc's position. (3) On defence, take away the first option, then help.

**What to tell players**

- **Read, process, move:** gather information (where's the force, where's the disc, who's the thrower), work out what it means, then act (HN Excel Premium, Game IQ).
- **Close isn't covered:** look at separation, hips and the defender's eyes, not just distance (HN Excel Premium, Fixing Focus).
- **Dangerous space:** the most dangerous throws are long vertical sprints into the endzone, but that changes with the disc's position. The defender's job is to spot genuinely dangerous situations and block those (BFD, Defensive fundamentals).
- **You have time to help:** an attacking flow usually involves three or four attackers and lasts about 20 seconds, so the other defenders can reposition, help or double (BFD).
- **When to poach:** is someone entering a space where they'll probably score? Is your player in a non-dangerous space? Is there a real chance of disrupting the offence? If yes, leave your player (HN Flik, When to Poach).
- **Flash the lane:** leave just long enough to deny one throw, then recover (HN Flik, Flashing the Lane; HN Team Defence).
- **Ask:** "How aware were you of your teammates' movements?" (Flik)

## Week 21 · 21 Feb · Retention under pressure: escaping the sideline

**Learning points:** (1) On the sideline, get the disc off early, before the defence settles. (2) The best reset is both off the line and upfield. (3) When trapped, cutters attack the break side and the middle.

**What to tell players**

- **Where to catch a sideline reset:**
  - Worst: behind the thrower and still on the line.
  - Upfield on the line: gains yards but leaves you trapped.
  - Backfield into space: gets off the line but loses yards.
  - Best: off the line and upfield, using the sideline wrinkle or sideline power position (HN Flik, Sideline Resets).
- **Don't look upfield for 4-5 seconds when trapped:** get it off the line straight away, before the defence is balanced (HB 36.3).
- **Punish the trap:** square up to the middle; cutters aim break side and middle (HN Handler Situations).
- **Know the shift:** a good defence will call "shift" at about stall 5 to shut the dump; the counter is to dump earlier (HB 36.4).
- **1s, vert from the front:** a third handler at the front of the stack gives an upfield reset. Throwing to them laterally gains yards and opens the break side (HN Flik, Upfield Dump).
- **Ask:** "In what spaces can the receiver easily get the disc? What does the team gain from the different options?" (Flik L3 Lesson 8)

- High-stall handler cuts: the dip (a 2 m pop in front of the disc) works at stall 8-9; the dump behind the disc is the last resort (HN Excel Premium).

## Week 22 · 28 Feb · Retention: quick disc processing and transitions

**Learning points:** (1) Catch ready to throw. (2) After a turnover, pick your target before you pick up the disc. (3) Clear the dead space around the disc.

**What to tell players**

- **Fast break principles:** identify a target before picking up, spread the field, and clear the dead space within about 3 m of the disc (HN Coach Corner, Fast Break Principles).
- **Picking up after a turn:** look upfield before you pick up. Then take the free yards, create disc movement, or wait and walk (HN Handler Situations).
- **Call the play early:** cutters need 4-5 seconds. Defenders on a turnover stop the easiest first throw and state the force twice, calmly (UAP Handler O and D, Wiggins, Transitions).
- **Fast break isn't always "throw immediately":** sometimes the best option is to get the disc to your best thrower and isolate your best receiver (HN Coach Corner, The Key to Fast Break Offence).
- **1s, ho in full:** peppermills (the two in front of the disc cut equal and opposite, then take each other's space); against a deep poach, run the banana (both handlers go upline to open a big swing) (HN Flik, Peppermills and Banana).
- **2s, vert in full:** cut from the back; time the cut to be open just before your teammate catches; the stack moves with the disc (HN Flik, Vertical Stack).

## Week 23 · 6 Mar · Solent warm-up week: game day, pull plays and the endzone

**Learning points:** (1) The same warm-up before every game. (2) The sideline is part of the team: buddies and talk. (3) Pull plays: know your first look and your backup.

**What to tell players**

- **Game-day warm-up:** throw, then throw and run, then the 4v3 mini (four offence against three defence, score and rotate out for quick reps). In the second half, let players prepare in their own way (HB App. A.3; Kieran).
- **Sideline buddies:** each player on the line has a buddy on the sideline who watches their matchup and talks to them (BFD, Team management).
- **Off the pull:**
  - The "1" just secures the pull, moving forwards onto it.
  - The "2" checks their shoulder, sets up where they can easily be hit, and centres it.
  - About 70% of the time, the disc should then go upfield to a cutter quickly (HN Handler Situations, Fielding the Pull and Being the 2).
- **Pull plays:** each has an ideal output (for example, a free deep cut for your best hucker) and a backup. When the play dies, move into your system by swinging the disc. Don't hang out in the deep space, and don't clog the space in front of the stack (HN Excel Premium, Dynamic Pull Plays and Transitioning to your System).
- **Endzone:**
  - Call "endzone" and set a vert stack. The first three players to arrive run to the back, the fourth stays at the front, and the other three handle (HB 26.5).
  - Come wide around the field rather than running through the lanes (HN Excel Premium, Converting in the Redzone). Our second endzone call is "Spread": three handlers work a dominator at the front of the endzone while the other four spread wide across the back. If their defenders sag in to clog the dominator, hit the spread receiver (Kieran; the same idea as HB 26.4, where cutters keep their defenders away at the back of the endzone).
- **Short hucks:** score before the defence recovers. If you can't, overload or isolate (HN Flik, Converting Short Hucks).
- **Solent:** set process goals for the day (for example, every player checks their shoulder five times a point). Use a short briefing and breathe together in the huddle (HN Amsterdam Conference; HN Case Studies, Mooncatchers 2024).

## Week 24 · 13 Mar · Defence for Tour 1 (1 of 2); squads announced

**Learning points:** (1) Intensity is a choice you make every point. (2) Zone is not half effort. (3) Echo every call.

**What to tell players**

- **Squads:** announce them with individual conversations, not just a list. Everyone gets their trial feedback and a development focus, and the pitch still applies: everyone improves, in both squads.
- **Solent review:** use the four-number stats (no-turn goals, breaks, conversion rate) and the process goals rather than the results (HN Coach Corner, Base Metric Analysis; HN Amsterdam Conference).
- **1s, the Bull zone (2-3-2):**
  - Two chases hold a middle force and stop easy resets.
  - The bull shuts down the middle.
  - Wings stop shots over the top.
  - The deep-deep and short-deep stop hucks.
  - "CRUMBLE" switches to match; echo it (HN Bull Zone).
- **Zone intensity:** zone is not half effort: running hard, attacking the disc and holding position all the time (HN Flik Zone Concepts).
- **Switching to match:** pick up the deepest offensive players first; when in doubt, leave the thrower unmarked (HN Flik, Transitions).

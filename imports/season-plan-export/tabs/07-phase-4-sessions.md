# Phase 4 sessions: Regionals

19 June to 30 July 2028 · weeks 38-43 · UKU Regionals (\~18-19 Jul) and EUCS Summer Tour (\~25-26 Jul, 1s only)

## How Phase 4 works

**What changes:** no new structures. The playbook from Phases 2-3 is sharpened, scouted and rehearsed until each squad can switch plans on a call. Sessions get shorter and sharper: high intensity, a little less volume, and they finish on time.

- **Joint session (about 2.75 hours in weeks 38-40):** starts with the 25-minute skill floor, because individual improvement doesn't stop for Regionals; the rest of the first hour is one fundamental refresher, chosen from the latest review; the rest is game-speed play and rehearsal.
- **Split session (about 2.5 hours):** built around scouting and Plans A, B and C, with the 4v4 conditioning block kept. It follows the same shape as before: a game, one drill or walk-through, the game again, 4v4, then a scrimmage.
- **Tests and focus time:** the skill floor is still each player's focus time. The mini-test in week 40 is the first 20 minutes of the skill floor. Mid-season one-to-ones (weeks 38-39) use the week 35 test results.
- **Everyone handles (2s):** the handling rules and the handler tally carry on through Regionals. The three handlers for each 2s O point are named before the event.
- **The 4v3 mini** stays as the warm-up finisher every week and at every event.
- **Heat:** July sessions need water breaks every 20-25 minutes, shade for the sideline and sunscreen; shorten the conditioning block on hot days rather than skipping water (Plan).
- **Fitness:** the 4v4 block ends with two sets of 4 x 20 m sprints with a cut in weeks 38-40, and the home programme reaches about 100 jump contacts in week 40. Both drop in the rehearsal week. The big peak comes 2-3 weeks before Nationals, in Phase 5. Hard leg sessions should not fall on consecutive days (Season plan, Fitness).

**Scouting**

CUSB put their 2019 EUCF result down to scouting: a team of scouts watched three or four games of each opponent, with two people watching the same game for different opinions, and wrote down every player's favourite throws and cuts. Then they trained how to escape the situations they didn't like (HN Case Studies, CUSB 2019).

- Each likely Regionals opponent gets two scouts from the squad, assigned in week 38.
- Scouts fill a one-page card: best players and their favourite throws and cuts; main offence and defence; what they do after a timeout; what they do in wind (HN Case Studies, Mooncatchers scouting sheet).
- Cards are presented in the week 40 split session, and printed for the sideline at Regionals.

**Triggers for switching (Plan):**

- **Defence:** switch after conceding two easy holds in a row, or when the opponent is scoring in fewer than five passes.
- **Offence:** switch after two turnovers in a row from the same problem.
- **Who decides:** the captains, at a timeout or between points. Plans are known before the game, so the call is one word (BFD, Game plans).

**Lines:** the 1s settle an O-line and a D-line by the end of week 40 (the 2s can if they want). The sideliner records O or D starts so stats split by line (HN Coach Corner, Base Metric Analysis).

**Mental skills:** big games bring nerves, and that's normal.

- **Process goals:** process goals that can be hit many times a game give feedback; outcome goals don't. For example, make them throw 10 passes before they score, or get 2 breaks every 6 points (HN Amsterdam Conference, Process over outcome).
- **Accept and refocus:** a flexible mind notices the nerves or the mistake, accepts it, and brings attention back to the next job, rather than trying to make the feeling go away (HN Coach Corner, Getting out of your head). The Inner Game of Ultimate calls the doubting, judging voice the Heckler and the trained, instinctive player the Hero: let the Heckler plan and review, and trust the Hero in games. Use a cue word such as "soft hands" or "patient and sharp" to get back to relaxed concentration (The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee).
- **Controllables:** "loose, but serious": focus on things you can control (HN Case Studies, Pony 2018).

## Week 38 · 19 Jun · Tour 3 review; breaking and cutting refresher; mid-season reviews

**Learning points:** (1) one fix from the Tour 3 review. (2) Cut early: the disc should move before the defence is set. (3) Every break throw goes straight into a continuation.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **Why stall 3:** CUSB played many scrimmages at stall 5 or even stall 3. Their players hated it, but it made cuts start earlier and the disc move before the defence set up, which beats poaches and junk sets (HN Case Studies, CUSB 2019).
- **Process over outcome:** Regionals is the outcome; this week's goal is something we can hit many times a session, like two breaks every six points (HN Amsterdam Conference, Process over outcome).
- **Mid-season reviews:** everyone, 1s and 2s, gets a short one-to-one in the next two weeks (Season plan, The 2s).

**What the games push players to do**

- **Break-throw bonus point (joint):** the fourth and hardest version: break, then a continuation within two throws, at stall 5. Every break has to go straight into flow. Ask: "Who was moving before the break was caught?"
- **3v3 scoring game (joint):** the offence scores 2 for every open-side catch and the defence 1 for a block. The defence has to take the open side away, so the offence learns to cut early and to the break side. Ask the defence: "What did you have to give up to block the open side?"
- **Short-stall 7v7 (joint):** stall 5, then stall 3. Nobody can wait: cuts start earlier and the disc moves before the defence is set, which beats poaches and junk. Keep it short and praise early cuts, not just goals. Ask: "When did your cut start, compared with a normal game?"
- **Plan A or B before each pull (1s):** the call is the constraint. Seven players hear one word and all do the same thing.
- **7v7 in vert at stall 5 (2s):** the same short-stall idea in the 2s' own offence.
- **Vert by call (2s):** "back" or "front" on a call, with three new handlers every two points.

**Harder / easier**

- **Break-throw bonus point:** harder: the break must lead to a continuation within two throws; easier: any break.
- **3v3 scoring game:** harder: a smaller pitch; easier: a bigger pitch.
- **Short-stall 7v7:** harder: stall 3 for the whole block; easier: stay at stall 5.
- **7v7 in vert at stall 5 (2s):** harder: stall 4; easier: stall 6 with a spare attacker.

**Coaching notes**

- Book the mid-season one-to-ones across weeks 38-39; the captains of each squad share them out. Use the player's week 35 test results, their stats and two clips, and agree one development focus for the summer. Start with three questions: what do you like most about Herd, what do you like least, and what would you change if you were captain? (The Culture Code)
- Short-stall games are hard and frustrating; keep them short and celebrate early cuts, not just scores.

## Week 39 · 26 Jun · Marking and switching marks; defence plans; 2s arrowhead in full

**Learning points:** (1) Triangle mark by default, flat mark on a call, changed straight away. (2) The mark's job carries on after the release: stop the give-and-go. (3) Know the defence plan and its trigger.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **The mark:** stay on the balls of your feet, shuffle rather than lunge, and keep hands low and unpredictable. Take away the dangerous throw first, then close. After the throw, stop the give-and-go (Phase 1 weeks 7-9).
- **Flat mark:** on the call, flatten to take away the around break; everyone downfield knows the open side has changed (BFD).
- **Straight after a timeout:** expect the opponent to run a set play; we can double-team the reset or poach the nearest throwing lane for three seconds (HN Coach Corner, Time Outs).
- **2s, arrowhead:** a wall in front of the disc, the chase forcing towards the line, and a line trap with two tips once the disc reaches the sideline. Don't get outflanked (HN Flik, Arrow Head; Flik Zone Concepts 6).

**What the games push players to do**

- **Tag prestall (joint):** stall 3 before the tag scores for the offence. The mark has to arrive at once, which is the habit the flat-mark call depends on.
- **Blocks Only (joint):** the hardest version: only a caught block counts. Defenders have to take a position, not a swipe. Ask: "What did you take away to get there first?"
- **7v7 with defence calls (joint):** the mark or the defence changes on a call, with a bonus for a stall or block after a change. The whole line has to know the open side has moved. Ask: "Who downfield heard the call? What did you change?"
- **8-pull with triggers (1s):** the defence is called before the pull and changed when a trigger is met. The captains practise spotting the trigger; the line practises the one-word switch.
- **5-pull against the arrowhead (2s):** the offence may only swing and crash. The wall learns its one job, not being outflanked, before hammers are allowed. Ask the wall: "Where did they nearly get round you?"
- **Arrowhead, then person D on a call (2s):** the 2s now have a wind defence and a way out of it.

**Harder / easier**

- **Mark-call drill:** harder: the call comes at stall 2; easier: the call comes before the stall.
- **Tag prestall:** harder: stall 6, and the mark must also hold the force; easier: no stall.
- **Blocks Only:** harder: only a caught block counts; easier: any touch counts.
- **5-pull against the arrowhead (2s):** harder: the offence may throw hammers; easier: the offence must swing three times first.

## Week 40 · 3 Jul · Retention under pressure; offence against the defences we expect

**Learning points:** (1) Only throw to open players. (2) Against poaches and junk, move the disc before the defence sets. (3) 2s: choose the vert by call.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **Escape what we don't like:** CUSB trained specifically to escape the situations they hated (poaches, junk zones, anything that stopped their flow), and so will we, using the scouting cards (HN Case Studies, CUSB 2019).

**What the games push players to do**

- **Variable stall count (joint):** the fourth time, with a wider stall-2 zone. The escape now has to work from further in. Ask: "How early did you have to turn to get it off the line?"
- **Retention game (joint):** a throw to a covered player scores for the defence even if it is caught, and a no-turn goal scores 2. The decision is scored, not the catch. There is no sprint: the joint session has no consequences. Ask: "Was the receiver open, or just close?"
- **Escape game (joint):** the defence plays the looks we expect at Regionals, and a no-turn score counts double. The offence practises getting out of the situations it likes least. Ask: "What was the look? What was our answer on the scouting card?"
- **Offence against the scouted defences (1s):** three points against each look. The answer to each is rehearsed until it is one call.
- **Out-of-timeout game (1s):** every possession starts from a timeout. Expect a poach or a double team on the reset; the play is built for it.
- **Sideline-start game with give-and-go (2s):** the 2s' sideline escape, at Regionals intensity.

**Harder / easier**

- **Variable stall count:** harder: widen the stall-2 zone to 8 m; easier: stall 4 in the zone.
- **Retention game:** harder: stall 6; easier: 7v6.
- **Escape game:** harder: the defence changes look mid-point; easier: the defence announces its look.

## Week 41 · 10 Jul · Game speed and full rehearsal

**Learning points:** (1) Everything at game speed. (2) Rehearse the Plan B and C triggers in a real game. (3) A reset routine after mistakes.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **Nerves are normal:** don't try to make them go away. Notice the feeling or the mistake, accept it, and bring your attention back to your next job on the pitch (HN Coach Corner, Getting out of your head).
- **A reset routine:** after a mistake, one breath and one cue word, then straight to your next job. That quiets the Heckler and hands back to the Hero (Plan; based on HN Coach Corner, Getting out of your head, and The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee).
- **Loose, but serious:** focus on what you can control: your effort, your next cut, your mark (HN Case Studies, Pony 2018).

# Thundering Herd Season Plan 2027/28

Oct 15, 2026 · @Kieran

The 2027/28 season rebuilds Herd around skills, not systems: every player in both teams gets measurably better, the 1s reach Nationals in September 2028, and the 2s win more games and stay a full part of the club.

## Philosophy

**The pitch:** Herd is a place where everyone gets better, whether you have played elite ultimate for ten years or picked up a disc a year ago.

Last season taught us that a systems-first year takes the fun out and leaves fundamentals untouched. Under pressure, it was individual skill that failed us: getting open, seeing the field, breaking the mark, keeping the disc. This season turns that around.

**Principles**

1. **Skills first, systems second.** We teach a shared base of fundamentals all season. Structures come late, and there are only a few of them.
2. **Learn it in the game.** Most training happens in small games whose rules push players towards a skill. This follows Ian French's constraints-led approach. Drills make up about 20-30% of a session, used when a skill needs isolating.
3. **One club, one curriculum.** The 1s and 2s learn the same language and the same skills. Difficulty changes, content does not.
4. **Everyone is measured on improvement.** Each player has baselines and personal goals. Progress is visible, not assumed.
5. **Fun is part of the plan.** Competition, scoring and variety keep sessions fun. In tricky decisions we choose the simplest and most fun option.
6. **Players solve problems.** Coaches ask questions more than they give answers, so players can adapt when a game doesn't go to plan.

## Goals

Each team has one headline outcome and a few process goals we control. The process goals are proposed starting points to agree with the captains.

|  | Outcome goal | Process goals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1s** | Qualify for Nationals, September 2028 | Fewer turnovers per point than 2026/27; at least 6 confident break throwers on the roster; every player improves on 3 of 4 benchmark tests |
| **2s** | Win more games than 2026/27, and enjoy it | Every 2s player handles in games; at least 2 players move up to the 1s during the season; every player improves on 3 of 4 benchmark tests |

The benchmark tests are set out in *Measuring improvement*.

## Skills curriculum

The curriculum is built around last season's four problem areas, plus the throwing and movement base they depend on. Each area grows from technique (Phase 1) to pressure (Phase 2) to team use (Phases 3-4).

| Area | Phase 1: Foundations | Phase 2: Under pressure | Phases 3-4: In the team | Key games and drills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Throwing and catching base** | Grip, balance, pivot range, IO/OI control, wide and low releases, clap catch | Throwing tired; quick release after catch; throwing to space | Hucks to space; throws chosen by wind and game plan | 70-pass; star throws; low releases under a pool noodle; jam drill; Archery; Ladder climb |
| **Cutting under pressure** | Acceleration, chop stop, change of direction, the three cut families (explosion, commitment, shoulder) | Beating a tight defender; timing cuts to start before the thrower catches; clearing as part of cutting | Cutting pairs and continuation within team structures | Five-cone cutting; gate drill; box of death; clear-cut drill; 1v1 "name your cut" |
| **Reading the pitch** | Active, inactive and ready space; making space vs taking space | Choosing the open player; scanning before the catch; changing the point of attack | Throwing progressions per role; game management by score and clock | Asymmetric offence; Golden Arm and Golden Hand; Honeypass; target number of passes; shot clock; Caterpillar and Space Hopper |
| **Breaking the mark** | Pivots, threat positions, fakes that look real | Breaking a tight or extended mark; off-balance throws; early IO vs late around | Breaks that lead to yardage-gaining continuation | Extended mark; one-crossover marking game; break-throw bonus point; break force game; score on break |
| **Retention** | Dump, mac and zip vocabulary; positive and negative resets; Five Frisbee Habits | Getting off the sideline; resets under a trap; only throwing to open players | Jedi (dump and swing that gains yards); endzone offence | Look-forward-once-then-dump; variable stall count; 3-handler game; Jedi lanes game; redzone continuous |

The **Five Frisbee Habits** (from DK's Bible) are the 2s' anchor and a useful reset for anyone: sprint your cuts; cut up and down, not across; check what's behind you; look for the dump by stall 5; only throw to players who are free.

## Weekly structure

Each week has one theme, announced in advance, with no more than three learning points. Both sessions serve it. The joint session teaches the theme to the whole club; the split session applies it at each team's level.

**Joint session (3 hours, whole club, mixed groups)**

| Time | Block | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Warm-up and footwork | Led by a rotating player; includes ladder or cone footwork |
| 0:20 | Focused throwing | Groups of 4-5 mixing 1s and 2s; one technique point of the week |
| 0:40 | Opening game | A small game that exposes the theme, e.g. variable stall count for sideline retention |
| 1:00 | Skill block | Short drills (8-12 min each) if the game shows a skill needs isolating; groups by skill level, not team |
| 1:30 | Water and questions | "What did you notice? Where was the space?" |
| 1:40 | Theme game, harder | Same game, tighter constraint; Honeypass pairs a 1s with a 2s player |
| 2:20 | Conditioned 7v7 | Rule that rewards the theme; teams mixed across 1s and 2s |
| 2:45 | Free game and recap | Last 10 min unconditioned; players say back the three points; shout-outs |

**Split session (3 hours, each team on its own)**

| Time | Block | 1s | 2s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Warm-up | Dynamic plus reaction work | Same |
| 0:20 | Throwing under pressure | Extended mark, off-balance throws | 3-person break drill, Get 10 |
| 0:40 | Theme: game, drill, game | Harder constraints, even numbers | More overload (5v4), more reps per player |
| 1:40 | Small-sided conditioning games | 4v4 continuous, stall 5 | 4v4 continuous, stall 7 |
| 2:00 | Team toolkit (from Phase 2) | Structures, set pieces, game-plan reps | Resets, endzone play, every player handles |
| 2:40 | Scrimmage and recap | Points with fun consequences for the losing line | Same |

Until squads are picked in March, split sessions run as two provisional groups based on last season's teams, and players can move between them. In Phase 1 they can also be split by skill level.

The week-by-week themes for both sessions are in Weekly plan.

## The 2s

The 2s get the same coaching quality, attention and visibility as the 1s. These are standing commitments, not good intentions.

1. **The 2s' own two captains** run the 2s sessions and sit in captains' meetings with an equal voice. The 1s captains help them plan sessions and share material, without taking over the running.
2. **Same curriculum, same theme.** The 2s learn exactly what the 1s learn each week; only difficulty and numbers change.
3. **Joint session time is shared fairly.** Mixed groups, Honeypass pairings and mixed 7v7 teams mean 2s players train with and against 1s players every week.
4. **Everyone handles.** In 2s games and scrimmages, rules such as "last player to touch the disc picks up after a turnover" and Golden Arm make sure no one is only a cutter.
5. **A clear pathway.** Criteria for moving up to the 1s are published at the start of the season. Players can move up mid-season; 1s players can drop in to support the 2s at tournaments.
6. **2s goals are celebrated.** 2s results, improvement scores and player awards are announced alongside the 1s in club updates and socials.
7. **Their own tournament plan.** The 2s have a target event and game plans prepared in the same way as the 1s, not left until the week before.
8. **Individual feedback for everyone.** Each 2s player gets the same mid-season and end-of-season review as a 1s player.

## Defence toolkit

The triangle mark is our default, and every player can switch to the BFD flat mark on a call. Team defences stay few and well drilled.

**Marks**

| Mark | When | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Triangle (default)** | Disc in the middle of the field; against throwers with big pivots | Hand takes the shown throw, body the around, shoulder the inside; step back to cover a big pivot, then close again |
| **Flat (BFD)** | Thrower is breaking well; disc in the power position; we want lateral throws only | Shoulders and hips parallel to the endzone; close in for the first threatened throw, then hold within 3 m |
| **Trap** | Disc on the sideline, for a few seconds | Close the line; dump defender takes away up the line |

The call comes from the sideline or a captain and can change mid-point. All marks share Robyn Wiseman's athletic marking base: wide stance, weight on the balls of the feet, hips back, eyes on the thrower, hands placed deliberately rather than waved.

**Team defences (from the end of Phase 2)**

- **Person defence (default, both teams):** back-shoulder positioning on cutters and hard-working forces.
- **Team defence awareness (both teams):** every player knows which spaces are dangerous for each disc position, and poaches or clogs the lanes near the disc to close gaps. This helps the 2s compete with stronger teams as much as it helps the 1s.
- **Called switches (both teams, from Phase 3):** switch on crossing cuts and picks, called early and by name; the caller decides, and new matchups hold until the next stoppage. The buzz switch covers a cutter who beats their defender deep (HN Team Defence, Switching against cutters; Flik).
- **Bull zone, 2-3-2 / 2-4-1 (1s; a basic version for the 2s):** Herd's existing "Bull" zone (HN Bull Zone). Two chases hold a middle force, allow backward swings, and stop easy resets and close give-and-goes. The bull sits just behind them and shuts down throws through the middle. Wings stop shots over the top; the far wing pinches in as a second bull but doesn't bite on the third handler. A deep-deep and short-deep stop hucks. The aim is lots of swings that gain nothing and bad throws into the wind. "CRUMBLE" is the call to switch to match, and everyone echoes it.
- **Arrowhead (1s, then 2s):** a wall in front of the disc denies the middle and forces to the sideline in wind, with a line trap (a chase and two tips) once the disc reaches the line (HN Flik, Arrow Head; Flik Zone Concepts 6). The 1s learn it in Phase 3; the 2s preview it then and take it on in full in Phase 4.

* **Force middle (1s option):** self-correcting on both sides, clogs the middle and works against big, fast teams. Call it early from the mark and the sideline. Success means at least one poach look per point and opponents needing about three more passes than against a one-way force (HN Point of Attack).
* **Team-defence skills to drill:** heads-up D, flash poaches, the buzz backfield switch, last back, switching against cutters and defending dishy moves (HN Team Defence; HN Next Gen 10).
* **Point-of-attack options:** anti-huck force, no-hammer mark and adjusting the mark to the matchup (HN Point of Attack).

The 2s focus on person defence with poaching and clogging, and play a basic version of the Bull zone. They preview the arrowhead in Phase 3 and take it on in full as their wind defence in Phase 4, once the 1s have it settled.

**Training it:** the BFD feedback exercise, Wiggins' one-crossover marking game, the 4-person break-force drill, Space Invaders scoring for priorities, and Ian French's Tag Prestall and Blocks Only games for intensity.

## Offence toolkit

Offence rests on a shared vocabulary and reset system taught to everyone before any structure; structures arrive in Phase 2, are built out in Phase 3, and stay few.

**Shared vocabulary (from BFD, taught in Phase 1)**

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Dump | Any pass behind the disc line |
| Mac | An upline cut in front of the disc, starting at or above the disc line |
| Zip | A vertical cut for a short throw on the move |
| Dynamic dump | A dump caught on the break side that starts quick flow there |
| Jedi | A dump and swing that gains yards: every yardage-losing throw is followed by a change of attack point |

Players call these out loud as they move. BFD's reset rule: a reset handler on the open side sets *negative* (behind the disc, about 45 degrees); on the break side they set *positive* (ahead, about 30 degrees).

**Handler rules (from Hildo Bijl)**

1. On the catch, throw forward or make a convincing forward fake, then look to the dump straight away.
2. If the disc risks getting stuck on the sideline, getting it off is the only priority.
3. Handlers time their cuts to be open the moment the thrower turns to them.

**Structures (Phases 2-3)**

- **Vertical stack**, cutting from the back or the front: the 2s' main offence and the 1s' second.
- **Horizontal stack**, with a **dominator** set from it: the 1s' main offence.
- **Zone offence** for both teams: going around, through and over the zone. Endzone calls: "Endzone" (a vert stack) and "Spread", where three handlers work a dominator at the front of the endzone while the other four spread wide across the back, ready to punish defenders who sag in to poach (Kieran).
- **Style model:** the Grut women's team. Collect clips of the patterns we want to copy, remembering their players are elite.
- Roles are defined by space, not position (BFD): any player can be handler or cutter depending on where they are.

**Every touch should do one of three things** (BFD): gain yards, change the point of attack, or break the mark.

## How we coach

We coach mainly through games and questions, in the spirit of Ian French's *Games for Coaching*, and accept that sessions will look a little messier than a drill-heavy plan.

**Game design**

- Pick a game whose rules reward the behaviour we want, e.g. variable stall count for sideline retention, break-throw bonus point for breaking the mark.
- Start simple with small numbers or an overload, then tighten the constraint as players succeed.
- Prefer games where teams score in an endzone; they transfer best to real ultimate.
- Use the one-possession format: many short reps, with natural pauses for a question.

**Talking to players**

- Ask before telling: "What did you notice? Where was the space? What would you do differently?"
- Stops last under 30 seconds and carry one idea.
- Give feedback that is specific, soon after the moment, and calm on the sideline (Hildo's ready → situation → what you did → what to try → check).
- Notice and name improvement out loud; specific praise moves players more than criticism.
- Experienced players coach within drills: each drill has a harder variation for those who are ready.

**Fun consequences (split sessions only)**

- Losing lines or teams run something useful: a 5-10-5 shuttle, a deep cut, or a rep of box of death.
- Scores are kept during the drill and settled at the end, so play is not interrupted.
- Consequences are for the group, not individuals, and stay light-hearted.
- The joint session has no consequences; it stays the welcoming session for the 2s and newcomers.

**Running sessions**

- Plans are written for a month at a time and shared, so anyone can step in.
- New drills are tried first with the captains and a few players before the whole club sees them.
- Sessions end with games and with players wanting more.

* **4v3 mini is the club's standard warm-up finisher**, at training and before every tournament game. Four offence play three defence on a small pitch; each four gets one possession (score or turnover), then rotates out for the next four, giving quick reps and lots of touches. Full rules are in the Phase 1 sessions tab.

**Classroom sessions:** about once a month, a 45-60 minute session online or in person takes one idea from *The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee*, *The Culture Code* or *The Captain Class* (or the Rise Up talks, Flik and BFD). Each is timed so the idea is used on the pitch the following week, and each is led by a rotating captain or player leader. There are twelve, from "How we learn" in October to the season review in September; the Classroom sessions tab has the full plan.

## Fitness without a track or gym

Fitness comes mostly from inside sessions, through small-sided games and short, game-shaped running, plus a simple bodyweight programme players do at home.

Ultimate is mostly repeated short sprints: roughly 5-6 maximal sprints of 10-15 m per point, at 90-95% of maximum heart rate (BFD). Training should match that rather than long runs.

| Method | Where | Dose | Phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small-sided games (3v3 / 4v4, \~30 m pitch, stall 5, continuous) | Split sessions | 2-3 blocks of 4-5 min | 2 onwards |
| Keeping margins (defender holds 2-3 m off a random runner) | Split sessions | 3 x 40 s on, 20 s off | 1 onwards |
| Repeated sprints with cuts (10-30 m, 20-30 s rest) | Split sessions, or as fun consequences | 2-3 sets of 4-6 | 2 onwards, building up |
| Agility and footwork (ladder or cones, reaction drills) | Warm-ups | 5-10 min, low volume, full rest | All |
| Bodyweight strength, landings, single-leg balance | At home, 2 x 20-30 min a week | Simple sheet with progressions | All |
| Plyometrics | At home or warm-ups | Start \~40 contacts, build towards \~100 | 2-4 |

**Timing:** general strength and base endurance in Phases 1-2; sprints, plyometrics and high-intensity games peak about 2-3 weeks before Nationals; then a 1-2 week taper. Hard leg sessions should not fall on consecutive days.

**For players with gym access:** the Flik Level 2 programme in the notebook is already periodised: off-season 1-2, pre-season 1-2, in-season and peak. Each block has accessory and stability, strength and power, and speed sessions. There is also a bodyweight, mobilisation and trunk circuit for anyone without a gym (HN Level 2 Strength and Conditioning). Train for intensity, not volume; maximal efforts need full rest (HN Coach Corner, Roster Size and Tryouts).

## Measuring improvement

Every player is tested three times (October, at the February trials, and September) on four quick benchmarks, so the pitch — everyone improves — has evidence behind it.

| Benchmark | Test | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throwing range and accuracy | Hucking alley: 10 hucks into a 20 m-wide lane; count in and record distance | UAP backhand hucks module |
| Breaking the mark | Get 10: 10 throws past a marker to a static receiver 10-15 m away; count completions | Flik Level 4 |
| Change of direction | 5-10-5 agility shuttle, filmed and timed | UAP changing direction module |
| Catching under pressure | 20 catches in a heads-on or overload drill; count clean catches | Hildo Bijl |

**Team stats (from Phase 3, filmed games where possible):** turnovers per point, completion rate, breaks thrown, discs received by opponents by zone (break / reset / open), and offensive yardage per player. At minimum, a sideliner records four things per point: score, O or D start, blocks and turnovers. From those, work out no-turn goals, breaks, conversion rate, perfect conversion rate, recovery and successful-defence rate (HN Coach Corner, Base Metric Analysis).

**Process goals:** alongside outcome goals (Nationals, wins), every player and team sets goals they can hit several times a session. For example: check your shoulder 5-6 times a point, give sideline talk three times a point, make the opposition throw 10 passes before scoring. These give feedback every week (HN Amsterdam Conference, Process over Outcome).

**Player reviews:** a short one-to-one at the start, middle and end of the season. Each player keeps a simple decision grid (situations where they turn it over, and what they'll try instead) and sets two personal goals. Players with a home throwing or fitness plan can follow a UAP module for four weeks.

## Leadership and roles

Two captains cannot coach 44 players well alone, so the plan adds a small leadership group with clear jobs. BFD recommends separate leads for offence, defence and team management; this adapts that to Herd.

| Role | Responsibility | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head coach / 1s captain | Season plan, weekly themes, joint sessions | Kieran |
| 1s captain | Team management, culture, selection with the head coach | Second 1s captain |
| 2s captains (2) | Run 2s split sessions; equal voice in captains' meetings; plan sessions with support from the 1s captains | 2s captains |
| Internal guest coaches | One-off sessions or blocks on a specialism (e.g. marking, handling, pulling) | Masters players and friends of the club |
| Skill leads (2-3) | Each owns an area (throwing, marking and defence, cutting) and runs that block | Experienced 1s players, to recruit |
| Warm-up and fitness lead | Warm-ups, home fitness sheet, benchmark testing | To recruit |
| Stats and video lead | Films games, keeps team stats, builds the clip library | To recruit |
| Comms and kit | Posts sessions in advance, kit, socials | To recruit |

**Sideline buddies:** at tournaments, players are paired (ideally similar roles) to coach each other from the sideline. Sideline talk is practised in training too, e.g. points where only players off the pitch may talk.

**Leading without ego** (HN Amsterdam Conference): a leader doesn't need to play well, they need to play hard. They don't need to know the answer, they need to find one. They don't need the right decision, they need a decision they then work with. And they don't need to love everyone, just the team.

**Playing and coaching at once:** plan practices and lines ahead, hand off admin, and use a whistle to switch between player and coach mode (HN Amsterdam Conference, A Balance Act). Share leadership roles across senior players rather than holding them at the top (HN Coach Corner, Shared Leadership).

**Tournament briefing:** before each event, share a short document covering objectives, scouting, lines, matchups and routines. Breathe together in the huddle, and play game by game rather than for the prize (HN Case Studies, Mooncatchers 2024).

**Choosing captains and player leaders** (*The Captain Class*): pick for behaviour, not talent or charisma. Look for doggedness, willingness to do the thankless jobs, constant practical chatter with everyone, the courage to argue about how the team plays (never personal), and emotional control under pressure. The full summary, and how *The Culture Code* and *The Inner Game of Ultimate Frisbee* shape our coaching, is in the Captains' and coaches' guide.

## Recruitment and the open off-season

The Phase 1 skills sessions double as the club's shop window: open to any player with some experience, fun from minute one, and visibly about getting better.

- **Advertise the pitch.** Posts lead with "get better at ultimate this winter", name each week's skill, and go out at least a day ahead.
- **Make newcomers' first session great.** A named welcomer, mixed throwing groups, and games with overloads so new players complete passes.
- **Experienced players host.** In games with newcomers, experienced players throw to them, don't poach and let them handle (DK's welcome-session rules).
- **Show progress early.** Newcomers do the benchmark tests in their first month and see their own improvement by March.
- **Clear next step.** Newcomers know how squads are picked and that the 2s are a real team with real goals.

**How trials run** (based on GB Open 2010 trials; HN Coach Corner, Tryouts):

- **Drills:** use game-like drills that are new to everyone, so nobody has a home advantage and you see who adapts.
- **Grading:** each assessor grades one attribute only (throwing, cutting or defence) to cut bias; average and rank the scores, then discuss them alongside selectors' notes.
- **Scrimmages:** run free scrimmages as well, so players can show their strengths.
- **Physical tests:** keep them simple: 5-10-5, a 50 m sprint with 5/10/35 m splits, and the arrowhead agility test (HN Testing).
- **More trial ideas** (HN Testing, Syc): circle throwing against one to three defenders; small-sided games with a team task; a communication funnel.

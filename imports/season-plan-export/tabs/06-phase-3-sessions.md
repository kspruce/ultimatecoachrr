# Phase 3 session plans

Session-by-session plans for the thirteen Early season weeks (20 March to 18 June 2028), from the run-up to UKU Tour 1 to UKU Tour 3. The playbook is now built and tested through a run of events: event weeks are light, and the week after each event fixes its top two problems. As before, the joint session comes first, then the 1s split and a suggested 2s split, with every drill sourced.

## How Phase 3 works

The rules from Phases 1 and 2 still apply: the source key and every-session rules (Phase 1 tab), the split-session shape with its 2:00 team-toolkit block (Phase 2 tab), and the 4v3 mini warm-up finisher. Squads are now picked, so splits are the 1s and 2s squads. The 2s may attend different events from the 1s; their captains slot their own event weeks into the same loop.

**The skill floor:** from week 25, every full joint session has a 25-minute skill floor straight after the warm-up, in place of focused throwing (Season plan, Weekly structure). 1s and 2s players work side by side in groups of three, so they learn from each other. Where the joint session used to open with a theme activity, the blocks before the water break are shorter to make room. Event weeks are the only exception.

**Tests and focus time in Phase 3**

- **Development focus:** the skill floor is each player's focus time, 12 minutes a week on their own focus and 12 helping their partner.
- **Mini-tests (weeks 27 and 32):** the first 20 minutes of the skill floor. Get 10 and the 1v1 cutting test are two of the skill-floor activities, so this is the skill floor with the scores written down.
- **Mid-season skill tests (week 35):** five stations of 12 minutes in the joint session. Each player then checks their development focus with a skill lead at the start of the split.
- **Wind before Tour 1:** the week 25 skill floor and the week 26 throwing block run into, with and across the wind.

**Split sessions in Phase 3** keep the Phase 2 shape: throwing, a game, one drill, the game again and harder, 4v4 continuous, a toolkit walk-through and game, then a scrimmage. The 4v4 block ends with one set of 4 x 20 m sprints with a cut (week 25), then two sets from week 32 (Season plan, Fitness). Every 2s scrimmage and toolkit game names one of the three handling rules (Phase 1 tab), and at events the 2s' stats owner adds the handler tally to the four numbers.

**Light weeks:** event weeks have a timed table too, with its length in the heading, so the session ends when it should.

**The event loop**

- **Event week:** a light session, with throwing, pulls, set pieces walked through and short games. Finish early.
- **Review:** after each event, use the stats sheet and any film to pick the top two problems.
- **Fix week:** the week after an event spends its theme blocks on those two problems, chosen from the fix menu below.
- **Judge events on process goals and how the concepts look, not results.** The aim is to be the team that scores the last point of the last game of the last tournament (HN Case Studies, Mooncatchers 2024).

## Week 25 · 20 Mar · Ready for Tour 1: defence and zones in the wind

**Why defence for Tour 1:** Mixed Tour 1 is usually very windy, and offence is hard in those conditions. Our goal for the event is defence: make every point hard work for the opposition and take the breaks the wind gives us. Offence stays deliberately simple, and the zone offence and endzone block moves to week 27 (Kieran).

**Learning points:** (1) Make them throw one more pass: in wind, every extra throw is another chance to turn it over. (2) Zones live on talk: count the attackers in your area and echo every call. (3) Win the deep space: read the disc early and box out.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **Defence is mitigation:** take away their first option and make them take the second and third. Most blocks come from frustration and fatigue, not highlight plays (HN Amsterdam Conference, Elite Defense Mental Shift).
- **Zones tempt bad throws in wind:** Brute Squad used the wind to tempt long throws into crowded spaces and prioritised downfield cutters, forcing lateral passes. Fury came back from 10-1 down in a windy 2008 final with a zone (Flik Deep Dives, Brute Squad Zone Defence; Fury v Riot 2008).
- **Pulls set up the defence:** in wind, bladey or rolling pulls are hard to field and give the defence time to set up. A pull that rolls out of the sideline can start a sideline trap (Flik Deep Dives, Brute Squad Pulls).
- **In unpredictable wind:**
  - Always mark a downwind thrower.
  - Make the offence throw into small spaces.
  - Expect hucks downwind and get bodies behind them (HN Deep Dives, Extreme Wind: Molly Brown v Phoenix).
- **1s, Bull zone or arrowhead by wind direction:**
  - The Bull zone (2-3-2) forces middle.
  - The arrowhead puts a wall in front of the disc, a chase and a line trap with two tips, and forces to the sideline in strong crosswind (HN Bull Zone; HN Flik, Arrow Head; Flik Zone Concepts 6).
  - "CRUMBLE" switches to person defence.
- **2s:** person defence with poach and clog near the disc, plus a basic Bull zone for upwind points.
- **Our offence in the wind** stays simple:
  - Big spaces and few throws.
  - Hucks downwind to an isolated cutter.
  - Big under cuts upwind.
  - Everyone backs up every throw (HN Deep Dives, Extreme Wind).

**What the games push players to do**

- **Updog (joint):** one attacker and one defender contest a high disc; the defence scores more for a catch than a block. It pushes both to read the disc early and take a position, and the defender to go for the catch. Ask: "When did you know where it would come down?"
- **Blocks Only, with a zone (joint):** only a touch wins the disc, and every point starts in a zone. A zone can't wait for a mistake here, so players learn that zone is not half effort. Ask: "Who was running hardest in that zone? What were they taking away?"
- **Asymmetric defence (joint):** five against four, and the defence can score. The spare defender practises poaching a lane and then attacking after the turn.
- **Conditioned 7v7 (joint):** a bonus for a turnover within the offence's first five passes, and for any stall-out. It rewards making them throw one more pass.
- **8-pull zone reps (1s):** a point for every swing that gains nothing, 2 for a turnover into the wind. The score tells the zone what it is for: lots of swings and a bad upwind throw. Ask: "How many attackers were in your area just then?"
- **7v6 in wind (2s):** a player short, with double points for an early turnover. In wind, clogging the lanes near the disc is enough to force the hard throw. Ask: "Which throw did we leave them? Was it the one into the wind?"
- **8-pull zone reps in the basic Bull zone (2s):** the same line defends eight pulls, so each player holds one role long enough to learn it.

**Harder / easier**

- **Updog:** harder: higher, floatier throws contested by two; easier: shorter throws.
- **Boxing out:** harder: both players jump from a running start; easier: nobody jumps.
- **Blocks Only, with a zone:** harder: the offence may use any throw; easier: the offence must make five passes before going deep.
- **Asymmetric defence:** harder: a bigger pitch; easier: a smaller pitch.
- **8-pull zone reps in the basic Bull zone (2s):** harder: the offence may throw over the top; easier: the offence may only swing and crash.

**Coaching notes**

- Deep defence happens rarely in games but is high-stakes, so it needs reps in training (HN Practice Sessions 12).
- **Tour 1 defence goals** (agreed at the week 24 squad meeting):
  - Make the opposition throw at least 10 passes per point.
  - Earn at least two breaks per game.
  - Concede no easy huck over the zone.
  - Chase every pull at full speed.
  - The stats owner adds blocks and passes per point to the four numbers (HN Amsterdam Conference, Process over Outcome; HN Coach Corner, Base Metric Analysis).
- **Toss and wind plan:** decide in advance. In strong wind, consider choosing defence and pulling downwind to chase an early break (HN Deep Dives, Firetails).
- Send the Tour 1 briefing this week, and confirm the stats owner and pullers.

## Week 27 · 3 Apr · Tour 1 fixes; zone offence around and through; endzone

**Learning points:** two set from the Tour 1 review, plus (3) Against a zone, swing the full width, drive the middle, and go around if it compresses. (4) Score from 10 m with the "Endzone" and "Spread" calls.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **Check-ins:** every player gets a 10-minute check-in this week or next, with their two Tour 1 clips and their trial test results: is the development focus still the right one?
- **The review:** share the stats (no-turn goals, breaks, conversion rate) and the two fixes. Attribute what went well to the whole group (HN Coach Corner, Base Metric Analysis and Fostering Team Identity).
- **Through the zone, with a controller:** one handler gets every second pass. It's easier than it looks if you throw and go, and it makes the space in front of the disc dangerous, so defenders either get overloaded or squeeze in and open space on the wings (Flik Zone Offence 2).
- **Crash the cup:** move into the space inside the cup for a short pass to reset the stall, gain yards or move defenders. If a crash isn't thrown to, get out, or you crowd the thrower (HN Flik, Crashing the Cup).
- **Drive, then decide:** if passes through the middle gain yards, keep going; if the middle is congested, the zone has compressed, so dump and swing around it (Flik Zone Offence 3). Going around: four handlers in a bucket keep the full width; throw swings with an OI curve, and catch and throw in one motion so the cup can't catch up (HN Flik, Dump and Swing; Flik Zone Offence 1). This block moved here from week 25 so the run-up to Tour 1 could focus on defence.

**What the games push players to do**

- **Fix games (joint):** each fix gets one drill and one game, chosen from the fix menu. Tell players what the review showed and which rule answers it, then ask: "What did the stats say we do? What does this rule make us do instead?"
- **Conditioned 7v7 (joint):** one rule that rewards the fixed behaviour at full spacing. If the behaviour disappears in the free game, the fix needs another week.
- **El Rondo (both):** four throwers keep the disc from a cup, and the thrower who turns it over joins the cup. The offence learns to take the open option the cup leaves; the cup learns where its holes are. Ask: "Which option did the cup leave open? Did we take it?"
- **Mini 3v3 with a controller (1s):** one handler must get every second pass. It forces throw-and-go through the middle of a zone, which makes the space in front of the disc dangerous. Ask: "What did the defenders do when the controller kept getting it back?"
- **Bucket and swing against a 2-3-2 (2s):** a point for a swing that reaches the far third. The 2s are rewarded for using the full width before trying to go through. Ask: "How far did the cup have to run? What opened when it was late?"
- **Endzone from 10 m (both):** a count out of 5. Conversion is the measure, not style.

**Harder / easier**

- **Fix blocks:** harder: use the levers in each fix-menu drill: lower stall, less space, an extra defender; easier: the opposite.
- **Bucket and swing (2s):** harder: stall 7 and a cup of three; easier: 7v6.
- **El Rondo:** harder: an 8 m square with a cup of three; easier: a 12 m square with a cup of two.
- **Mini 3v3 with a controller (1s):** harder: stall 5; easier: one passive defender.
- **Endzone from 10 m:** harder: 5 out of 5; easier: 3 out of 5.

**Coaching notes**

- **Mini-test:** the first 20 minutes of the skill floor (the 0:30 row).
- Fix-week rule: no more than two fixes, and back each one up in a conditioned game, or players revert (DK).
- In El Rondo, if the cup is set up wrong, an option should be wide open; throwers should take it so the cup learns where the holes are (HB 31.2).

## Week 28 · 10 Apr · Hucking to space and deep cutting (Easter, lighter)

**Learning points:** (1) Set up the huck first, with a swing or power position. (2) Huck to the break side, over the defender's shoulder, with OI. (3) Deep cutters wait for power position, start shallow, and look sideways rather than back.

**Background (say two at most)**

- **Ring's deep game:**
  - Swing to the break side.
  - An offset lateral cut from the open side takes an inside pass.
  - The isolated cutter turns deep, and the huck goes over their defender's shoulder with OI, caught on the break side (HN Deep Dives, Ring of Fire Hucking).
- **Break-side fade:** lead the huck slightly to the break side; it's easier for the cutter to read and lets them box out (HN Excel Premium, Improve your Hucking).
- **Hucking past a mark:** catch in power position and throw before the mark sets; pump-fake one side and huck the other. Against a flat, no-huck mark, add OI and aim outside the receiver (HN Excel Premium, Hucking with a Mark).
- **Consistency:** in games, wind up to about 75% of your pulling range (HN Excel Premium, Backhand Huck).
- **Deep cutting:**
  - Wait for power position and start shallow (HN Next Gen 10, Deep Cut Timing).
  - As a deep runner, glance sideways at the disc rather than looking back at the thrower (HB 27.4).
- **Sprinting:** lean forward, pull the rope, throw back the bag of sand, look at the horizon (HB 27.3).

## Week 31 · 1 May · UKU Tour 2 (\~2-3 May): pulling and the pull chase

**What the games push players to do**

- **Shot clock game (joint):** score within 30 seconds or lose the disc. The clock pushes players to move the disc before the defence is set. Ask: "What did you stop doing when the clock was on?"
- **Minimum-time game (joint):** no score before 90 seconds. Now the same players have to keep it, reset the shape and wait. Ask: "How did you keep the disc without going backwards?"
- **Target number of passes (joint):** a bonus for scoring in exactly the target. Teams have to manage a possession on purpose, which is tempo.
- **Conditioned 7v7 (joint):** "fast" or "slow" called before the pull. The pace is chosen, not accidental.
- **Zone offence with an over-the-top bonus (1s):** a hammer or blade over the cup to an open receiver scores extra. The bonus only pays when the three conditions are met: open target, clean release, no float into a wing. Ask: "Was that planned, or desperate?"
- **3H against person D (2s):** only cuts away or across may be thrown to. The rule teaches the shape: cutters start close and leave, and the upfield dump keeps the disc moving. Ask: "Where did the space open when the cutters went away?"
- **Vert by call (2s):** "back" or "front" before each pull. Two verts, chosen by one word.

**Harder / easier**

- **Throwing over the cup:** harder: an active cup; easier: a static cup.
- **Shot clock game:** harder: 20 seconds; easier: 45 seconds.
- **Minimum-time game:** harder: 120 seconds at stall 6; easier: 60 seconds.
- **3H against person D (2s):** harder: live defence on the handlers; easier: half pace.

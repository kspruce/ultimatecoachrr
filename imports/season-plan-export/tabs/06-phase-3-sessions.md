# Phase 3 session plans

Session-by-session plans for the thirteen Event-build weeks (20 March to 18 June 2028), from the run-up to UKU Tour 1 to UKU Tour 3. The playbook is now built and tested through a run of events: event weeks are light, and the week after each event fixes its top two problems. As before, the joint session comes first, then the 1s split and a suggested 2s split, with every drill sourced.

## How Phase 3 works

The rules from Phases 1 and 2 still apply: the source key and every-session rules (Phase 1 tab), the split-session shape with its 2:00 team-toolkit block (Phase 2 tab), and the 4v3 mini warm-up finisher. Squads are now picked, so splits are the 1s and 2s squads. The 2s may attend different events from the 1s; their captains slot their own event weeks into the same loop.

**The event loop**

- **Event week:** a light session, with throwing, pulls, set pieces walked through and short games. Finish early.
- **Review:** after each event, use the stats sheet and any film to pick the top two problems.
- **Fix week:** the week after an event spends its theme blocks on those two problems, chosen from the fix menu below.
- **Judge events on process goals and how the concepts look, not results.** The aim is to be the team that scores the last point of the last game of the last tournament (HN Case Studies, Mooncatchers 2024).

## Week 25 · 20 Mar · Ready for Tour 1: defence and zones in the wind

**Why defence for Tour 1:** Mixed Tour 1 is usually very windy, and offence is hard in those conditions. Our goal for the event is defence: make every point hard work for the opposition and take the breaks the wind gives us. Offence stays deliberately simple, and the zone offence and endzone block moves to week 27 (Kieran).

**Learning points:** (1) Make them throw one more pass: in wind, every extra throw is another chance to turn it over. (2) Zones live on talk: count the attackers in your area and echo every call. (3) Win the deep space: read the disc early and box out.

**What to tell players**

- **Defence is mitigation:** take away their first option and make them take the second and third. Most blocks come from frustration and fatigue, not highlight plays (HN Amsterdam Conference, Elite Defense Mental Shift).
- **Zones tempt bad throws in wind:** Brute Squad used the wind to tempt long throws into crowded spaces and prioritised downfield cutters, forcing lateral passes. Fury came back from 10-1 down in a windy 2008 final with a zone (Flik Deep Dives, Brute Squad Zone Defence; Fury v Riot 2008).
- **Pulls set up the defence:** in wind, bladey or rolling pulls are hard to field and give the defence time to set up. A pull that rolls out of the sideline can start a sideline trap (Flik Deep Dives, Brute Squad Pulls).
- **In unpredictable wind:**
  - Always mark a downwind thrower.
  - Make the offence throw into small spaces.
  - Expect hucks downwind and get bodies behind them (HN Deep Dives, Extreme Wind: Molly Brown v Phoenix).
- **1s, Bull zone or arrowhead by wind direction:**
  - The Bull zone (2-3-2) forces middle.
  - The arrowhead puts a wall in front of the disc, a chase and a line trap with two tips, and forces to the sideline in strong crosswind (HN Bull Zone; HN Flik, Arrow Head; Flik Zone Concepts 6).
  - "CRUMBLE" switches to person defence.
- **2s:** person defence with poach and clog near the disc, plus a basic Bull zone for upwind points.
- **Our offence in the wind** stays simple:
  - Big spaces and few throws.
  - Hucks downwind to an isolated cutter.
  - Big under cuts upwind.
  - Everyone backs up every throw (HN Deep Dives, Extreme Wind).

## Week 27 · 3 Apr · Tour 1 fixes; zone offence around and through; endzone

**Learning points:** two set from the Tour 1 review, plus (3) Against a zone, swing the full width, drive the middle, and go around if it compresses. (4) Score from 10 m with the "Endzone" and "Spread" calls.

**What to tell players**

- **The review:** share the stats (no-turn goals, breaks, conversion rate) and the two fixes. Attribute what went well to the whole group (HN Coach Corner, Base Metric Analysis and Fostering Team Identity).
- **Through the zone, with a controller:** one handler gets every second pass. It's easier than it looks if you throw and go, and it makes the space in front of the disc dangerous, so defenders either get overloaded or squeeze in and open space on the wings (Flik Zone Offence 2).
- **Crash the cup:** move into the space inside the cup for a short pass to reset the stall, gain yards or move defenders. If a crash isn't thrown to, get out, or you crowd the thrower (HN Flik, Crashing the Cup).
- **Drive, then decide:** if passes through the middle gain yards, keep going; if the middle is congested, the zone has compressed, so dump and swing around it (Flik Zone Offence 3). Going around: four handlers in a bucket keep the full width; throw swings with an OI curve, and catch and throw in one motion so the cup can't catch up (HN Flik, Dump and Swing; Flik Zone Offence 1). This block moved here from week 25 so the run-up to Tour 1 could focus on defence.

## Week 28 · 10 Apr · Hucking to space and deep cutting (Easter, lighter)

**Learning points:** (1) Set up the huck first, with a swing or power position. (2) Huck to the break side, over the defender's shoulder, with OI. (3) Deep cutters wait for power position, start shallow, and look sideways rather than back.

**What to tell players**

- **Ring's deep game:**
  - Swing to the break side.
  - An offset lateral cut from the open side takes an inside pass.
  - The isolated cutter turns deep, and the huck goes over their defender's shoulder with OI, caught on the break side (HN Deep Dives, Ring of Fire Hucking).
- **Break-side fade:** lead the huck slightly to the break side; it's easier for the cutter to read and lets them box out (HN Excel Premium, Improve your Hucking).
- **Hucking past a mark:** catch in power position and throw before the mark sets; pump-fake one side and huck the other. Against a flat, no-huck mark, add OI and aim outside the receiver (HN Excel Premium, Hucking with a Mark).
- **Consistency:** in games, wind up to about 75% of your pulling range (HN Excel Premium, Backhand Huck).
- **Deep cutting:**
  - Wait for power position and start shallow (HN Next Gen 10, Deep Cut Timing).
  - As a deep runner, glance sideways at the disc rather than looking back at the thrower (HB 27.4).
- **Sprinting:** lean forward, pull the rope, throw back the bag of sand, look at the horizon (HB 27.3).

## Week 30 · 24 Apr · EUCS Spring (\~25-26 Apr): playing in wind

**Learning points:** (1) Use the wind: low release upwind, high downwind, more spin. (2) In gusty wind, make bigger throws into bigger spaces, and fewer of them. (3) Back each other up.

**What to tell players**

- **Throwing in wind:** release low going upwind and high going downwind, with extra spin (DK 4.9).
- **When the wind is unpredictable:**
  - Huck more, especially downwind to an isolated cutter, and set the huck up with a swing first.
  - Play in big spaces and slow the tempo, so there are fewer throws.
  - Get bodies under every disc to back up.
  - Get off the downwind sideline.
  - On defence, always mark a downwind thrower (HN Deep Dives, Extreme Wind: Molly Brown v Phoenix).
- **Downwind like the Firetails:** high releases over the mark are easy; an OI edge slows a huck down; cutters can sit deeper, which opens big flat under cuts (HN Deep Dives, Firetails Downwind Offence).
- **Crosswind:** the upwind sideline is the "high side" and the downwind one the "low side"; swings towards the high side are harder but worth more (Flik Zone Offence 8).
- **1s, arrowhead:** a wall in front of the disc denies the middle; swings are allowed, and the job is not to be outflanked. Add a line trap with the chase and two tips (HN Flik, Arrow Head; Flik Zone Concepts 6).
- **The toss:** decide the wind choice in advance; the Firetails chose defence to chase an early break (HN Deep Dives, Firetails).

## Week 31 · 1 May · UKU Tour 2 (\~2-3 May): pulling and the pull chase

**Purpose:** a light session built on pulling, then walk-throughs and Plans A and B for Tour 2.

**What to tell players**

- **The aim of a pull:** put the offence in the worst possible position. That means deep, with hang time so the defence is set, ideally near the back of the endzone or rolling out of the sideline. Only aim at the brick if you can't reach further (HN Flik, Pulling Strategy).
- **Shapes:** OI turns down and stays controllable; IO flattens for hang time but can slide out; the double helix gets the most hang time but needs the best edge control. Throw diagonally across the pitch rather than out and back (HN Flik, Shapes of Pull).
- **Technique:**
  - Pick a target first.
  - Use a relaxed run-up with a cross-step, and pull the disc back in a straight line ("a starter motor, not a discus").
  - Push hard through the front foot.
  - Have a routine (HN Flik, Pulling Technique).
- **Training:** little and often, and when tired; specialise rather than everyone pulling; take a few pulls each way before every game (HN Flik, Training Pulling).
- **The chase:** everyone knows the coverage, and the chasers call early whether the disc is central or on the sideline (HN Flik, Pulling Strategy; Flik Zone Concepts 6).
- **Plans A and B:** know your team, the opposition, the conditions and the field, and have Plans A, B and C ready before you need them (BFD, Game plans).

## Week 32 · 8 May · Fix and deepen: breaking the mark; dominator against poaching defences

**Learning points:** one from the Tour 2 review, plus (2) the extended mark makes game marks feel easy, and (3) against poaches, move the disc between handlers and punish the space the poacher left.

**What to tell players**

- **Why the dominator beats poaches:** the cutters clear vertically, leaving lots of space for one-on-one handler play (BFD, Dominator).

## Week 33 · 15 May · Switching on defence; Bull zone blocks (1s); poach and clog against stronger offences (2s)

**Learning points:** (1) Switch only on a clear call, early and loud. (2) The defender who is beaten calls for help; the helper takes the cutter, and the beaten defender takes the helper's player. (3) Poaching is a team job: if one person poaches, everyone shifts.

**What to tell players**

- **When to switch:** switch on crossing cuts and picks, when your teammate is closer to your player's next space than you are. Call it early, use the player's name, and both defenders commit (HN Team Defence, Switching against cutters).
- **Three rules for downfield switches:**
  - The defender who calls the switch decides.
  - Never switch on a cutter who is already moving at full speed away from you.
  - After a switch, the new matchups stay until the next stoppage, unless someone calls again (HN Team Defence, NG downfield switching).
- **Buzz switch:** when a cutter beats their defender deep, the nearest defender "buzzes" across to take them, and the beaten defender takes the open player (Flik).
- **Help the helper:** when a teammate is beaten, your job is to make their mistake cost nothing (IF).
- **1s, Bull zone:** the bull's job is to make the throw ugly, not to get the block. The blocks come from the wings and the middle reading the forced throw. Count the attackers in your area every time the disc moves.
- **2s, poach and clog:** better offences than ours run cuts through the middle. Sit in their lanes and make them throw around us; our person D isn't beaten until they've completed the second throw (HN Flik, Disruptive Poaching).

## Week 34 · 22 May · Tempo; zone offence over the top (1s); vert from the front (2s)

**Learning points:** (1) Choose the pace on purpose: fast to beat a set defence, slow to reset the shape. (2) A hammer or blade over the cup is a planned throw, not a desperate one. (3) 2s: in a vert from the front, cutters start close to the disc and cut away.

**What to tell players**

- **Tempo:** a game with a shot clock teaches you to keep moving; a minimum time teaches patience. You want both (IF).
- **When to throw over the zone:**
  - Throw a hammer only when the target is open in the space behind the cup, you have a clean release, and the throw doesn't float into a wing (Flik Zone Offence 9).
  - Blades and scoobers are quicker over the cup; hammers go further.
- **Making space for the over-the-top throw:** deep cutters drag the deep defenders back, which opens space in the middle (Flik Zone Offence 9; HB 33.3).
- **2s, vert from the front (3H):**
  - Three handlers at the back; cutters start close to the disc.
  - Cuts go away from the disc or across the pitch, using offset lateral cuts.
  - The upfield dump keeps the disc moving (SP 3H cutting; Flik Upfield Dump).

**Learning points:** (1) Only throw to open players. (2) Have a game plan for each opponent you know about. (3) Plan for things going wrong before they do.

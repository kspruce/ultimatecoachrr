How the player ratings work

Where PER, EPM and Eff come from, what scoring efficiency (SE) is, and the sums the site does in the background. Your team's own numbers are shown {%- if season %} for the {{ season.name }} season{% else %} for every game you've recorded{% endif %}.

In short
  • SE (scoring efficiency) is the share of possessions that end in a goal, counting both teams. It sets how much a turn costs.
  • Every goal, assist, block and turn is converted into goal equivalents (GE) using SE.
  • EPM is a player's total GE: a fairer plus/minus.
  • PER is GE per {{ PER_SCALE }} points played. It shows how much a player gives the team per point, with small samples pulled towards the team's rate.
  • Eff is between 0 and 1. It shows how much of what a player produces they keep, rather than give back in turns.

1. Why not plain plus/minus?

Plus/minus is goals + assists + blocks − turns. It treats every turn as costing exactly what a goal is worth, whoever throws it and however your games are going. That's rarely true:

  • In a windy game, or at a level where the disc changes hands a lot, possessions are cheap. Losing one costs less, because the next one probably wouldn't have ended in a goal either.
  • In a clean game where most possessions end in a goal, every turn is expensive.
  • Handlers throw the most, so they take most of the risk. Plain plus/minus punishes them for it, especially in women's and mixed play, where turns are more common.

The ratings on this site fix that by pricing a turn according to how often possessions actually end in a goal.

2. Scoring efficiency (SE)

SE = goals ÷ (goals + turns)

Every possession ends in one of two ways: a goal or a turn. So SE is the chance that a possession ends in a goal. It counts both teams, because it describes the game being played rather than either side.

Where it comes from

SE and the way it's used here come from Better Box Score Metrics (BBSM), Paul Würtztack's stats column on Ultiworld. His main metric, EDGE, prices turns and blocks by the scoring environment. His EDGE Plus/Minus (EPM) and Eff apply the same idea to plain goals-assists-blocks-turns box scores, like USA Ultimate's. At the 2026 Club Nationals, SE was about 0.59 in the men's division and lower in mixed and women's. A turn in the women's division cost almost 30% less than in the men's.

BBSM works SE out per game for the semi-pro leagues, and per division for a whole tournament. This site works it out from whichever games you're looking at: the season on the dashboard, one tournament, or one game on a game's page.

How the site counts it

  • Goals: every point with full stats that ended in a goal, for either team.
  • Turns: every recorded change of possession, by either team: throwaways, drops, stalls, blocks, forced and unforced turnovers, and Callahans.
  • Points only recorded live on Game Day (just the line and the result) are left out, because they don't record the turns.
  • Points whose possession changes don't add up are left out too (see below).
  • With no recorded points yet, the site uses {{ DEFAULT_SE }}, which is about where club Nationals sits.

Points that don't add up

You don't need to record the other team's stats for SE. When they lose the disc, the stat taker has to press block, forced turnover, unforced turnover or Callahan to get back on offence, and that press counts their turn. If a point skips that step, its turns go missing and SE comes out too high. Turns would then look more costly than they are.

Possession alternates, so the result of a point fixes how its possession changes have to balance. On an O point we held, every turn of ours needs a regain to match it. A D point we scored needs one more regain than turns. A point that doesn't balance is missing something, so it's left out of SE (players' own stats from it still count).

3. Goal equivalents

Each play is converted into goal equivalents (GE) using SE:

PlayWorthFor you now (SE {{ n3|format(se) }})
Goal0.86 − 0.43 × SE{{ n3|format(coeffs.goal) }}
Assist1.14 − 0.57 × SE{{ n3|format(coeffs.assist) }}
Block+ SE{{ n3|format(coeffs.block) }}
Turn (throwaway, drop, stall)− SE−{{ n3|format(coeffs.turn) }}
Callahana goal and a block{{ n3|format(coeffs.goal + coeffs.block) }}

Why these numbers

  • A turn costs SE, and a block earns SE. When the disc changes hands, what changes is the chance of scoring with it, and that chance is SE. The scarcer goals are, the less any one possession is worth.
  • Goals and assists share out the rest, so that across a team the goals and assists minus the turns add up to the goals the team actually scored. Per goal there's one goal and one assist, worth 2 − SE between them. The turns behind that goal cost (1 − SE). The difference is exactly 1.
  • Assists are worth a bit more than goals. Throwers handle the disc more and take more turnover risk, and assists track that higher throwing volume better than goals do.

4. EPM: EDGE Plus/Minus

EPM = goals × G + assists × A + blocks × SE − turns × SE

EPM is a player's total goal equivalents for the games you're looking at. Read it like plus/minus, but with turns and blocks priced properly. As a total, it grows with playing time: it's the player's overall contribution.

5. PER: Player Efficiency Rating

PER = {{ PER_SCALE }} × (EPM + {{ PER_PRIOR_POINTS }} × team rate) ÷ (points played + {{ PER_PRIOR_POINTS }})

PER is goal equivalents per {{ PER_SCALE }} points played, so players who play different amounts can be compared. Points played only counts points with full stats.

The team rate is your team's goal equivalents per point played, over every player {%- if has_games and team_avgs.get('player_count') %}: {{ n3|format(team_avgs.avg_ge_per_point) }} for you, which is a PER of {{ '%.1f'|format(PER_SCALE * team_avgs.avg_ge_per_point) }}{% endif %}. Every player starts as if they'd already played {{ PER_PRIOR_POINTS }} points at that rate. With only a few points played, PER stays close to the team's rate. As points add up, the player's own numbers take over. So one great or terrible point doesn't put someone top or bottom of the table.

  • Above {{ '%.1f'|format(PER_SCALE * team_avgs.get('avg_ge_per_point', 0)) if has_games else 'the team rate' }}: more value per point than the average player on your team.
  • Below zero: turns are costing more than goals, assists and blocks bring in.

Earlier versions of PER were scaled so that the team average was 15. They also gave a little credit for completions, catches, hockey assists and on-field plus/minus. That's gone: PER is now in goal equivalents, the same units as EPM.

6. Eff: efficiency

Eff = plus GE ÷ (plus GE + turn GE + prior)

Plus GE is the goals, assists and blocks part of EPM. Turn GE is the turns part. Eff is the share of what a player produces that they don't give back. A player with no turns at all would approach 1. Unlike EPM and PER, Eff doesn't reward volume: it rewards clean play.

The prior is the average player's turns for these games, priced at SE {%- if has_games and team_avgs.get('player_count') %} ({{ n3|format(team_avgs.eff_prior) }} for you){% endif %}. Without it, someone with one goal and no turns would score a perfect 1 and top the list. With it, Eff only gets high for players who produce a lot and turn it over little. BBSM does the same: Peter Boerth's 10 goals, 15 assists, 1 block and 0 turns at 2026 Club Nationals came out at 0.931, the best in the men's division.

8. What counts, and what doesn't

  • Only points with full stats count, the same as the site's other per-point rates.
  • Hockey assists, completions, catches, throwing distance and break throws aren't part of the ratings. They're still on every stats page. BBSM treats goals and assists as a sample of a player's wider involvement, and keeping the ratings to goals, assists, blocks and turns keeps the books balanced.
  • Defence without a block (marking, poaching, forcing a bad throw) and offence without a goal or assist (cuts that open space) don't show up in any box score. Use the ratings to ask questions, not to settle them.
  • Each rating uses the SE of the games you're looking at. A player's PER for one game is priced by that game's SE, so it can differ from their season PER even at the same rate.
